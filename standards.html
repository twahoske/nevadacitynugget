Who Owns the Paper

The Nugget is independently published in Nevada City by the owner of The Hat Store, 314 Broad Street. It is a volunteer effort: the paper earns no money and takes no funding of any kind, from sponsors, investors, grants, political groups, or government. It is not an official publication of the City of Nevada City or Nevada County, and has no relation to the historical paper of the same name beyond admiration.

The ads. The Hat Store advertisement in every edition is the publisher's own shop. Now and then the paper also runs a courtesy ad for a friend's business or a community cause. No one pays for any ad, every ad is plainly an ad, and no advertiser has any say over the news. When a business that has run a courtesy ad turns up in a story, the story says so.

When the paper covers its own. If The Hat Store, its block of Broad Street, or a matter in which the publisher has a personal stake appears in the news, the story says so plainly. The Hat Store will appear rarely as Merchant of the Day, and with that disclosure when it does.

On the air. The publisher co-hosts Under the Influence on KVMR 89.5 FM. The Radio Log lists the station's whole day as KVMR publishes it, and the paper's coverage of the station is held to the same standard as everything else.

No One Pays to Appear

No business, venue, candidate, or organization pays to be listed, profiled, reviewed, or covered. Merchant of the Day, the Amusements listings, and every story are chosen on merit and on what can be verified. No gifts or free goods are accepted in exchange for coverage. If the paper ever sells advertising, every paid ad will be marked "Advertisement," kept apart from the news, and given no say over what the news reports.

How the Paper Is Made

Each edition is compiled with the help of an AI assistant (Claude, made by Anthropic), working early each morning, again at evening, and on watch for emergencies, from the public sources listed on Sources & Methods and under the publisher's standing rules. Features and photographs from the publisher's own reporting are spliced in as written. The publisher sets the rules, reads the paper, and answers for it.

The rules the assistant works under are the paper's rules: report only what a source supports, link every story to that source, never invent a quote, a vote, a showtime, or an outcome, say plainly when something could not be confirmed, and print corrections when wrong.

Accuracy and Fairness

Sourced or it doesn't run. Every story ends with an italic line naming, and where possible linking, what it was built from. Meeting summaries come from the official recording or agenda packet; votes are reported exactly as recorded.

Officials on the record. What elected officials are quoted as saying comes from the public record, with a date and a link.

The blotter. Retold in the paper's own words, and nobody is named unless charged or named in an official release.

Quotation. Other papers' reporting is summarized in the Nugget's own words, credited and linked, with at most a short quotation from any one source.

Right of reply. Anyone written about who believes the paper got them wrong or left out their side is invited to write news@nevadacitynugget.com.

Invented content. The horoscope, and only the horoscope. Recipes and illustrations are original to the paper; its imagery is its own drawings, the house ad, and photographs used with permission.

Corrections

When the paper gets something wrong it says so. The item is fixed and a Corrections box at the bottom of the next edition (or of that evening's edition) states what was wrong and what is right. Mistakes are never quietly rewritten, and archived editions stay as they were published. To report an error, write news@nevadacitynugget.com with the edition date and the item.

Tips and Submissions

Tips are welcome and every one is checked against independent sources before it prints; a tip runs as "a reader tip" unless the sender asks to be named. Events from reader flyers are confirmed with the venue or organizer and credited "from a reader flyer." Reader photographs, recipes, and nominations run only with permission, and Citizen o' the Day only with the honoree's consent. See the Privacy page for how what you send is handled.

Accessibility

The paper should be readable by everyone. It is plain web pages with real headings and text, alternative text on images, a layout that fits phones, and colors for both light and dark settings. If something gets in your way, from a screen reader stumble to type too small to read, write news@nevadacitynugget.com and it will be fixed.
