What It Is

A Gold Rush Town Keeps a Victorian Christmas

The Nevada City Chamber of Commerce has staged Victorian Christmas every year since 1978, on two Wednesday evenings and three Sunday afternoons in December. The idea is simple and it works: close the old downtown to through traffic, fill it with carolers in Victorian dress, artisan vendors and street food, keep the shops open late, and let the town's real gas lamps do the lighting.

It is the biggest draw of the year downtown, which is why the practical part of this guide matters. Plan the parking before you plan the outfit.

Source: Nevada City Chamber of Commerce, Victorian Christmas page.

Getting Here

From Sacramento and the Bay Area: Interstate 80 east to Auburn, then Highway 49 north through Grass Valley to Nevada City. From Reno and Truckee: Interstate 80 west, then Highway 20 west over the ridge into town. Either way you arrive on the freeway that runs past downtown; the Broad Street exit puts you at the bottom of the festival.

Do not park on the ramps. The Chamber warns that cars left on the on and off ramps of Highways 49 and 20 will be ticketed and may be towed.

Mountain weather. Nevada City sits in the Sierra foothills, and December storms can bring chain controls to the higher roads, Highway 20 and Interstate 80 especially. Check Caltrans before you leave: chain controls and winter driving.

On a tour bus? Buses load and unload only at the Broad Street overcrossing and must park off site; there is no bus parking at the event.

Sources: Nevada City Chamber of Commerce, Victorian Christmas page; Caltrans.

Parking & Shuttles

New for 2026: paying to park downtown

The City of Nevada City plans to charge for special-event parking in the most sought-after downtown spaces during Victorian Christmas. City staff said so at the November 12 council meeting, adding that downtown business and City Hall staff should park at Pioneer Park or elsewhere. Prices, hours and a map were not out as of November 14, 2026; we will add them here.

Source: Nevada City Council, November 12, 2026, staff report near the end of the meeting (Meetings on the Record).

Downtown parking is extremely limited and mostly on the street, and Broad and Commercial streets close to through traffic during the event. The Chamber's advice is to leave the car at a shuttle lot.

Bodhi Hive Shuttle

Park at 420 Providence Mine Rd.

What to See & Do

  1. The gas lampsEvery one of downtown's working gas lamps gets a swag of fresh local cedar and pine, made by Boy Scout Troop 24, with bows and flags, and close to 500 feet of fresh cedar garland hangs from the balconies. String lights do the rest.
  2. The town treeA 25-foot locally sourced tree, trimmed the old way with red bows and frosted pine cones, in Robinson Plaza, 132 Main St.
  3. Father ChristmasA Santa out of Christmases long past, walking the streets and spreading cheer. Sundays only.
  4. SantaKids can visit Santa and have their picture made.
  5. The Walking Christmas Tree and the Toy SoldierThey wander the event; stop them for a photo. Rain keeps the tree home, so look for her on clear days.
  6. Free roasted chestnutsRoasted over an open fire at the pit in the Food Court. Watch, then try one.
  7. The Dickens VillageThe Chamber was given a big collection of Department 56 Dickens Village models; they fill the windows of the Alpha Building, 210 Broad St.
  8. More than 100 artisan vendorsHandmade and local goods up and down Broad, Pine, Commercial and Union streets, while the boutiques stay open late.

The Chamber is still posting each day's entertainment lineup: carolers, brass, bagpipes and the rest. Find it on the Chamber's Victorian Christmas page under Entertainment Schedule.

Source: Nevada City Chamber of Commerce, Victorian Christmas page.

Eat & Drink

The Food Court

Every food vendor sets up in the Food Court on Commercial Street, which is also where the chestnuts roast. Downtown restaurants serve inside and on their patios; the Chamber keeps a restaurant list.

Hot Drinks on Broad

The Chamber's bar booth pours hot buttered rum, hot apple toddies and spiked hot chocolate (it calls them Hot Chocolate Snugglers). The VFW booth pours hot mulled wine. Both stand on Broad Street near Pine.

Bringing the Kids

  • Visit Santa and get a picture.
  • Balloon animals, next to the bounce house.
  • Magic and juggling shows on the stage at the corner of Broad and Pine (times on the Chamber's schedule).

Good to Know

  • Rain or shine. Extreme weather can cancel a day; see the Chamber's cancellation policy.
  • Dogs are allowed on a leash, the Chamber says. Crowds are thick on Broad; small dogs fare best.
  • Shoes. Downtown is steep, and some sidewalks are old brick. Wear soles that grip when it's wet.
  • Selling at the event? The Chamber takes vendor applications: vendor information.
  • Questions go to the Nevada City Chamber of Commerce, (530) 265-2692, info@nevadacitychamber.com.

Source: Nevada City Chamber of Commerce, Victorian Christmas page.

Quick Questions

When is Victorian Christmas 2026?
Five days in December 2026: Sundays December 6, 13 and 20 from 1:30 to 6 PM, and Wednesdays December 9 and 16 from 5 to 9 PM, in downtown Nevada City.
Where should I park for Victorian Christmas?
Downtown parking is very limited, and Broad and Commercial streets close to through traffic. The Chamber runs shuttles from the Rood Center, 950 Maidu Ave., and from Bodhi Hive, 420 Providence Mine Rd. The city says it plans to charge for special-event parking downtown this year, with details to come.
How much does the shuttle cost?
The Chamber lists $8 for adults, free for children under 15, free for anyone in Victorian costume, and free for every return trip.
Can I bring my dog?
The Chamber says the city allows dogs on a leash, and small leashed dogs that fit on a lap may ride the shuttle.
Does it happen in the rain?
Yes. It is a rain or shine event, though the Chamber says extreme weather can cancel a day.
