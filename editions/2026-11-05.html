Two days on, from the Capitol down to Broad Street. California counts for weeks, so close races here may not settle until later in the month.

What It MeansThe night in plain English

Bottom lineDemocrats have won both the U.S. Senate and the House, the AP says, and will run Congress come January. Xavier Becerra will be California's next governor. Here at home, Measure Q is passing comfortably in the county's count, with late mail and provisional ballots still to be counted.

  1. The SenateDemocrats win

    The AP has called the Senate for Democrats, who now hold 54 of the 100 seats to the Republicans' 44, with Alaska and one other seat still to be called. Troy Jackson unseated Susan Collins in Maine and Abdul El-Sayed held Michigan. From January Democrats will set the Senate's agenda, chair its committees and decide which of the president's nominees, from judges to Cabinet picks, get a vote.

  2. The HouseDemocrats win

    The AP called the House for Democrats at 10:24 PM Tuesday; by Wednesday morning they had 234 seats, well past the 218 needed, to the Republicans' 139. With both chambers, Democrats will control what bills reach the president's desk for the last two years of his term, and can open investigations of the administration.

  3. CaliforniaBecerra wins

    The AP has called the governor's race for Xavier Becerra, who leads Steve Hilton 64 to 36 percent with 59 percent of the vote in; he takes office Jan. 4. Prop. 37 (home buyer loans) passes and Prop. 39 (voter ID) fails, per the AP. Prop. 40, the one-time tax on billionaires, trails 45 to 55, and Prop. 5 leads by a hair, 51 to 49.

  4. Close to homeFirst count in

    Measure Q leads 61 to 39 percent in Nevada City after the county's election-night count. If it holds, short-term rentals in town move from a simple registration to a city permit with tighter rules. In the county's count, Bera leads Tucker 59 to 41 and James leads Hadwick 56 to 44, but those districts stretch well past the county. Mail ballots postmarked by Election Day still count if they arrive by Nov. 10.

City Hall & Politics

City Council · Schedule

Council Moves Its November Meeting To Thursday, Nov. 12

Veterans Day pushes the second-Wednesday session back a day · 6:30 PM in the Council Chambers, 317 Broad St. · The Nov. 25 meeting is cancelled · No agenda posted yet

The Nevada City Council will hold its only regular meeting of November on Thursday, Nov. 12, at 6:30 PM, a day later than usual. The city's online calendar now lists the regular Wednesday, Nov. 11, date as "Moved City Council Meeting to Thursday November 12, 2026," and shows a Thursday City Council Meeting in the Council Chambers at City Hall, 317 Broad St., that evening. City Hall itself is closed Wednesday for Veterans Day. The calendar also marks the fourth-Wednesday meeting, Nov. 25, the eve of Thanksgiving, as cancelled, so Nov. 12 is the council's one scheduled session until December.

What it will take up. Not yet known. The city's Agenda Center still shows nothing newer than the October 28 council agenda, and the minutes of that meeting have not posted either. Those minutes should settle whether the council formally voted on new City Hall public hours (open through the lunch hour and closed Fridays, starting Dec. 1); The Union reported the change as adopted, while the Nugget's caption transcript of the recording does not show a roll-call vote. The Docket will digest the Nov. 12 agenda the morning after it posts.

A full Thursday. The council shares the date with the county's public 1 percent hand count of the Nov. 3 ballots (9 AM, Loma Rica Drive), the county Planning Commission (1:30 PM, Rood Center), and the city's own Active Transportation and Trails workshop, which runs 4:30 to 6:15 PM at the Miners Foundry and lets out a quarter hour before the gavel.

Sources: City of Nevada City calendar, Nov. 11 meeting moved, Thursday City Council Meeting, Nov. 12, Nov. 25 meeting cancelled, City Hall closed for Veterans Day; Agenda Center, checked Nov. 5; The Union, Oct. 31; the Nugget's caption transcript; trails workshop listing.

Election · Measure Q (updated 5 PM)

Measure Q Keeps Its Lead Into The Late Count

Measure Q, the short-term rental measure, finished election night ahead 602 yes to 378 no, 61.4 percent to 38.6 percent, with all four city precincts reporting in Nevada County's final election-night count, posted at 12:24 AM Wednesday. That is up from the 546 to 308 tally in the 9:58 PM count reported here yesterday. A simple majority decides it. If the lead holds, short-term rentals inside city limits move from today's registration rules to a city permit with tighter eligibility and operating rules. Countywide, 29,155 ballots had been counted out of 76,215 registered voters, about 38 percent; mail ballots dropped off Monday and Tuesday were not in that count, and ballots postmarked by Election Day still count if they reach the county by Tuesday, Nov. 10.

Next. The county's first post-election update comes Friday at 4:30 PM, then every Tuesday and Friday at 4:30 PM. The public hand count is Nov. 12; certified results come no earlier than Nov. 25, and the county must certify by Dec. 3. The full board, from Congress to the propositions, is in Election Returns at the top of the page.

Still to count (5 PM). Nevada County has 18,650 ballots left to count on top of the 29,155 counted election night: 16,500 received on or before Election Day, 450 received since, 50 forwarded from other counties, 50 provisional and 350 same-day-registration provisional ballots, and 1,250 damaged ballots or ballots needing review; 304 ballots are awaiting signature cure. The county gave no Nevada City breakdown, so how many of those ballots could move Measure Q is not yet known. Updated, still unofficial results are due Friday afternoon. The 1 percent hand count is Thursday, Nov. 12, at 9 AM at the Ballot Processing Center, 12740 Loma Rica Drive, Grass Valley, open to the public.

Sources: Nevada County Elections results page, final election-night count as of 12:24 AM Nov. 4, as logged on the Nugget's Election Returns board; Nevada County Elections, Nov. 2 (count schedule, hand count, certification); YubaNet, Nov. 4 (final election-night release); YubaNet, Nov. 5 (ballots remaining); City Attorney's Impartial Analysis.

County · Veterans

Bigger Property Tax Break Coming For Disabled Veterans

The county Assessor says a new state law, SB 296, signed Sept. 28, expands the disabled veterans' property tax exemption starting with the Jan. 1, 2027 lien date, covering 50 or 100 percent of the first $1 million of assessed value depending on eligibility and income. For 2026 the basic exemption is $180,671 and the low-income exemption $271,009. No action is needed yet; current recipients get a mailing at the end of December. Questions: (530) 265-1232.

Source: Nevada County Assessor, Nov. 4

Merchant of the Day

No. 4 · Café & Bistro · 401 Commercial Street

Lika's Quarter Café: A French Accent On Commercial Street

Croque monsieur and shepherd's pie at the corner of York · The patio that came with the old Ike's · More than twenty years of San Francisco restaurant work behind it

The café at the corner of Commercial and York Streets was Ike's Quarter Café for years. When Ike decided to sell, Malika Nonomura, who goes by Lika (say "LEE-kuh"), took over the space with George Nonomura, drawn in good part by the outdoor patio. She called it "the best patio," The Union reported.

The cooking. The menu is a different animal from Ike's, a blend of California cooking and French bistro dishes. Nonomura told The Union she is proudest of the Croque Monsieur, the Croque Madame, and the Hachis Parmentier, a French take on shepherd's pie, and the menu fills out with salads, burgers, and sandwiches. She came to Nevada City after more than 20 years at Angelina's in San Francisco's Richmond District, where she took classes in cheese, catering, and running a dining room. The aim, per The Union, is good ingredients, comfortable portions, fair prices, and no pretension.

When to go. The Union reported breakfast and lunch service with dinner on Fridays, Saturdays, and Sundays. The Nugget did not find a current posting of hours, so check before you count on supper.

Pay a visit: 401 Commercial Street, at York Street, Nevada City. Hours not posted online that the Nugget could find; check before you go.

Arts & Culture

Courtesy Notice · Promotional · 210 Main Street, upstairs over Treats

Twelve Artists Fill The Red Dot For A Winter Art Market

Nov. 6 through Dec. 20 at the Red Dot Gallery on Main · An artist-owned gallery above Treats · Open Thursday through Sunday, 1 to 5

Climb the stairs above Treats at 210 Main Street and you reach the Red Dot Gallery on Main, a small, artist-owned gallery that opened downtown this spring. From Nov. 6 through Dec. 20 the gallery will fill its walls with the work of 12 artists for a holiday Winter Art Market, according to the gallery's flyer.

Who's behind it. The gallery's three principal artists are Jerianne Van Dijk, known for watercolor worked with ink; Kathryn "Kathy" Wronski, who paints mostly in oil, makes colorful, whimsical prints, and answers to "the Dog Lady" for her dog portraits; and Peggy Wright, a former art educator and gallery owner who also owns Treats downstairs. The Union reported in April that the artists mind the gallery themselves in rotation and keep a corner workspace where visitors can watch a painter at work. Van Dijk told the paper she likes to "smell the color."

About that name. In the art world, a red dot beside a painting means it has sold, and the artists told The Union they picked the name out of optimism. The paper also described a vending machine, Wright's idea, that drops a tiny 3-by-3-inch painting by one of the artists, chosen at random.

Pay a visit: Red Dot Gallery on Main, 210 Main Street, Nevada City, upstairs over Treats; enter through the ice cream shop. Thursday through Sunday, 1 to 5 p.m. The flyer adds Victorian Christmas hours during the market: Wednesdays 5 to 9 p.m. and Sundays 1 to 5 p.m. Hours are as the gallery posts them; check before you go.

Hey Good Lookin', Whatcha Got Cookin'

Deer Creek Stout And Onion Soup With Sharp Cheddar Toast (a Nugget original). Slice four large yellow onions thin and cook them slowly in three tablespoons of butter with a pinch of salt in a heavy pot over medium-low heat, stirring now and then, for a good 40 minutes, until they are soft and the color of an old saddle. Stir in two minced cloves of garlic and a teaspoon of fresh thyme leaves, then a tablespoon of flour, and cook a minute more. Pour in a cup of stout, scraping up the brown bits, and let it bubble down by half before adding five cups of beef or mushroom stock, a bay leaf, a teaspoon of Worcestershire, and a few grinds of black pepper. Simmer 25 minutes, fish out the bay leaf, and taste for salt; a spoonful of brown sugar softens a bitter stout. Meanwhile toast thick slices of country bread, pile them with grated sharp cheddar, and slide them under the broiler until the cheese bubbles. Ladle the soup into bowls and float a toast on each. Serves four, or two people and one Hamlet intermission.
