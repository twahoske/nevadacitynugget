Weather

Weather · Tonight Through Early Friday (updated 5 PM)

The Storm Arrives Tonight: Wind, Heavy Rain, And Mountain Snow

Dry and cold today · Rain and wind spread in after dark · Wind Advisory, gusts to 50 mph, 10 PM tonight to 4 PM Wednesday · Up to 2 inches of rain in the foothills by early Friday · Winter Storm Warning for the Sierra above 4,000 feet

Today is the dry day between storms, and the weather service in Sacramento says the second one is the stronger of the two. Its latest discussion the Nugget could read this morning, issued at 1:08 PM Monday, has rain spreading across the region tonight as the wind picks up; heavier rain Wednesday morning, a brief lull early that afternoon, then another wave Wednesday evening into Thursday, with rain continuing into early Friday. It forecasts up to 2 inches in the Valley and foothills from this evening through early Friday, more in higher terrain, moderate to heavy at times, with slick roads, ponding, and minor nuisance flooding. Caltrans, in its own storm notice, puts the foothills at up to 3 inches. OpenSnow has Nevada City at 46 today, then highs of 41 Wednesday and 42 Thursday.

Wind. A Wind Advisory covers the Sacramento Valley, the Delta, and the foothill zones from 10 PM tonight to 4 PM Wednesday, with gusts up to 50 mph and the strongest winds expected Wednesday. The weather service's hazards page for western Nevada County's own zone listed no active product when the Nugget checked this morning. PG&E's power shutoff page shows no new activity; its last event ended Oct. 17.

Snow, and how low. Snow levels start at 3,500 to 4,500 feet tonight, well above Nevada City at 2,477 feet, and rise to 4,500 to 5,500 feet Wednesday. The Winter Storm Warning for the northern Sierra and southern Cascades above 4,000 feet runs 10 PM tonight to 4 AM Friday: 1 to 3 feet of snow above 5,000 feet, locally 4 feet on the highest peaks, up to a foot between 4,000 and 5,000 feet, a dusting possible as low as 3,500, and snowfall rates of 1 to 2 inches an hour at times. "Travel could be very difficult to impossible," the weather service says of the mountains through early Friday.

Raised at 11:10 AM. The weather service reissued the Winter Storm Warning late this morning with bigger numbers: 2 to 4 feet of snow above 5,000 feet, up to 5 feet on the highest peaks, up to 20 inches between 4,000 and 5,000 feet, and a dusting to a few inches possible as low as 3,500 feet. Gusts in the warning area could reach 60 mph, enough to bring down branches and cut visibility. Snow levels run 3,500 to 4,500 feet tonight and about 4,000 to 5,500 feet Wednesday, with the heaviest snow Wednesday morning into the afternoon at 1 to 2 inches an hour or more. The warning still runs 10 PM tonight to 4 AM Friday; Nevada City, at 2,477 feet, remains below the forecast snow levels.

Thunder. Isolated thunderstorms are possible Wednesday afternoon and evening and again Thursday, likeliest Thursday afternoon and evening in the southern Sacramento Valley and the adjacent foothills, with lightning, small hail, gusty winds, and a low chance of a funnel cloud in the stronger cells.

If you must cross the hill. Caltrans asks drivers to avoid the Sierra during the storm if they can. Those who go should fill the tank, carry chains, food, water, and blankets, stay four car lengths behind snowplows, and stay on the highway rather than following GPS around chain controls. Conditions are on Caltrans QuickMap or at 1-800-427-7623.

Today, while it is dry. Clear gutters and drains. The county's free self-serve sand is at Highway 49 and East Broad Street (bring a shovel and bags), and the county holds a free sand and winter preparedness event at its warehouse there Wednesday, 1:30 to 4 PM. The weather service says unsettled weather may continue into next weekend and the week after, with below-normal temperatures possible.

Sources: NWS Sacramento, Area Forecast Discussion, 1:08 PM Nov. 16; the weather service's hazards page for western Nevada County, checked this morning; Caltrans via YubaNet, Nov. 16; YubaNet Happening Now, Nov. 16; OpenSnow, Nevada City; PG&E newsroom; Nevada County, Nov. 11; the reissued Winter Storm Warning, NWS Sacramento, 11:10 AM Nov. 17, as reposted by DefconLevel.

Evening Update · Power

PG&E Pre-Stages Crews For Wednesday's Wind And Rain

PG&E has crews pre-staged in the area ahead of the storm and expects some lengthy outages for a number of Nevada County customers Wednesday, KNCO reported this evening. Paul Moreno of PG&E's communications office told the station the company uses forecasting tools to decide where to place crews so restoration can start sooner, and noted that heavy rain, not just wind, can knock out power. His advice: charge phones tonight, set out flashlights with fresh batteries, and freeze extra ice to move into the refrigerator if the power goes. This is outage preparation, not a planned shutoff: PG&E's power shutoff page still shows no new event.

Sources: KNCO, "PGE Gears Up For Power Outages"; PG&E newsroom, checked 5 PM.

City Hall & Politics

Board of Supervisors · Today, 9 AM (updated 5 PM)

Supervisors Take Up Warming Shelter And Dispatch Pact Today

The county Board of Supervisors meets at 9 this morning, and two items on its consent calendar reach directly into Nevada City, on the day the storm arrives. One renews Sierra Roots' contract to run a warming shelter for Grass Valley and Nevada City, up to $75,000 for Oct. 1 through June 30. The other is a five-year public safety dispatch agreement among Grass Valley, Truckee, Nevada City, and the county, running to June 30, 2031. Board chair Lisa Swarthout will proclaim November Arts and Health Month. At 10 AM the board hears an appeal by the Cattle Drive Industrial Park Owners Association of a U-Haul expansion near Rough and Ready; staff proposes denying it. At 1:30 PM it takes up a forestry equipment storage yard and rezoning for Mountain F Enterprises. The meeting is in the Board Chamber of the Rood Center, 950 Maidu Ave.; comment by phone during the meeting at (530) 270-3474. Item-by-item details are in The Docket.

After the meeting. The board approved the $22,833 Title III plan, Secure Rural Schools money that buys mobile and handheld radios, fire hose, nozzles, and hose packs for the Washington Fire Department, the county said this afternoon. "As remote as Washington is, our closest help is often 30 minutes or more away," Fire Chief Mike Stewart said in the county's release. The county also released its 2025 crop report: a record $31.3 million in non-cannabis farm products, up 9 percent, plus an estimated $13.9 million in cannabis, about $45.3 million combined. The outcomes of the warming shelter contract, the dispatch agreement, the U-Haul appeal, and the Mountain F rezoning are not yet confirmed.

Sources: Board of Supervisors agenda, Nevada County Agenda Center; Nevada County News Flash, Washington Fire funding and 2025 agricultural report, Nov. 17; KNCO, Nov. 17.

Evening Update · County Code Compliance

County Floats A Public Nuisance Ordinance; Comments Due Dec. 16

Nevada County has proposed a Nuisance Abatement Ordinance meant, in the words of Code Compliance Director Matt Kelley, "to help the county more clearly define and address property conditions that can threaten health and safety." Illegal dumping, improper waste disposal, and other environmental hazards could count as public nuisances. Kelley told KNCO the county will still lead with education and voluntary compliance, and the ordinance would give it more to work with on serious, recurring violations when that fails. Public comment is open until Dec. 16, after which staff will weigh revisions. KNCO's report does not say how to submit comments; the county's Code Compliance office is the place to ask. The ordinance would apply to the county's jurisdiction; the report does not address Nevada City.

Source: KNCO, "Nevada County Proposes Public Nuisance Ordinance," Nov. 17.

Wildfire Preparedness · Countywide

Fire Safe Council Of Nevada County Files For Chapter 7 Bankruptcy

The Fire Safe Council of Nevada County, which closed its doors in August, is filing for Chapter 7 bankruptcy, its board announced Monday afternoon, ending 28 years of operation. Board Chair Pete Williams called it "the only realistic option" after several years of struggling to pay the bills. A court-appointed trustee will oversee the case and sell the organization's assets for its creditors; the board does not expect the process to finish this year, and the announcement does not say what the council owes. Board volunteers say they will keep sharing defensible space and Firewise Communities information; requests for a Defensible Space Advisory Visit or Firewise help go to firesafehelp@gmail.com.

Source: Fire Safe Council of Nevada County via YubaNet, Nov. 16.

Merchant of the Day

No. 16 · Restaurant & Tavern · 111 North Pine Street

Sherwood, Long Known As Friar Tuck's: Fifty-Three Years At Pine And Commercial

Open since 1973 in an 1860 brick building · Rebuilt after a 2002 fire · Live music Tuesday, Friday and Saturday, no cover

Week three continues at the corner of Pine and Commercial, where the restaurant long known as Friar Tuck's has been serving since 1973, by its own account. After about fifty years under that name it now goes by Sherwood Restaurant & Tavern, which it calls a new chapter with the same kitchen and the same owners, though its website still lives at friartucks.com.

The building. A landmark 1860 brick building on the National Register of Historic Places, with its original brick walls, a custom mahogany bar, and two wine cellars. The restaurant says it expanded its kitchen and bar and added a full liquor license in 1982, and that a fire gutted the place in 2002; it credits firefighters, local agencies, artists, neighbors, and guests with getting it reopened.

What's on. A seasonal menu built around Sierra Foothill sourcing, an extensive wine list that has won a Wine Spectator Award of Excellence 19 years running, cocktails, whiskey flights, and 12 craft taps. Live music plays Tuesday, Friday, and Saturday with no cover; tonight it is John Voelz, 6 to 9 PM. There are also screens for the game and a back room, the Sherwood Forest Room, for private events.

Pay a visit: 111 North Pine Street, Nevada City. Bar opens 4 PM daily except Wednesday; dinner from 5 PM (the site lists closing at 8 or 9 depending on the night). (530) 265-9093. friartucks.com. Hours are as the restaurant posts them; check before you go.

Feb. 18 to 22, 2027 · Nevada City & Grass Valley

Arts & Culture · Looking Ahead

Wild & Scenic Turns 25: The Film Festival Comes Back Feb. 18 to 22

The 25th annual festival picks "Momentum" as its theme · Early-bird passes on sale · The art call closes Nov. 30

SYRCL's Wild & Scenic Film Festival, the environmental film festival that fills downtown Nevada City and Grass Valley every winter, has set its 25th annual edition for Thursday, Feb. 18, through Monday, Feb. 22, 2027, according to the festival's website. For the anniversary it has chosen "Momentum" as its guiding theme.

The festival. SYRCL, the South Yuba River Citizens League, was founded in 1983 by activists working to protect the South Yuba from dams, and it produces the festival. The festival's own page describes a flagship event of more than 100 films, held each year in Nevada City and Grass Valley, before Wild & Scenic goes on tour to more than 125 locations. The 2027 films and venues have not been posted yet; the Nugget will run them when they are.

Passes. Early-bird passes are on sale now, and the festival is marking the 25th with "$25 off." Buy passes from the festival's ticket page.

Artists, last call. The festival's art exhibition, run with Nevada County Arts, takes submissions until a final deadline of Nov. 30: up to three pieces, all for sale, in 2-D, 3-D, or photography. Chosen work hangs in the Granucci Gallery at The Center for the Arts in Grass Valley, Feb. 18 to March 14, 2027, and at the Nevada County Government Center in Nevada City, Jan. 18 to April 30, 2027. Details and submissions; questions to art@wildandscenicfilmfestival.org or 530-264-7031.

Got a spare room? The festival is looking for locals to host a filmmaker or special guest during the festival, and for volunteers. Lodging host questions go to the festival office, (530) 265-5961, ext. 232.

The Nugget runs this as a community notice; no one paid for it, and the paper has no ties to the festival. Engle Wants Gold Coins Back In Your Pocket

Rep. Clair Engle introduced a bill to put gold coins back into general circulation, the Nugget reported from Washington, arguing a return to hard money would revive the gold mining industry hit hard by wartime restrictions. The bill would have let the president set the gold price above a floor of $35 an ounce, created a new $50 coin, and given miners the option of taking gold coin for their bullion. Gold country, it is safe to say, was in favor.

Hey Good Lookin', Whatcha Got Cookin'

Brown-Butter Skillet Cornbread With Honey (a Nugget original). Heat the oven to 425 and put a 10-inch cast-iron skillet in it while it warms. In a small pan, melt a stick of butter over medium heat and keep swirling until it smells nutty and the bits on the bottom turn the color of a walnut shell, then take it off the heat. In a bowl, whisk a cup of cornmeal, a cup of flour, a tablespoon of baking powder, half a teaspoon of baking soda, and a teaspoon of salt. In another, whisk two eggs with a cup and a quarter of buttermilk and three tablespoons of honey, then stir in all but two tablespoons of the brown butter. Fold wet into dry just until no flour shows; lumps are fine. Pull out the hot skillet, swirl the reserved butter around it, pour in the batter (it should sizzle), and bake about 20 minutes, until the top is golden and a toothpick comes out clean. Serve wedges warm with more honey, alongside whatever is simmering on the stove. It is best eaten while the wind is rattling something outside.
