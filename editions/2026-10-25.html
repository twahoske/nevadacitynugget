City Hall & Politics

The Week Ahead · City Hall

A Fuels Town Hall Monday, A Full Council Wednesday

Deer Creek canyon treatment explained at the Foundry, Monday at 6 · Council takes up regional dispatch, sewer-plant controls, and its own meeting hours · Mail ballots by Tuesday

City Hall's week opens Monday, October 26 at 6:00 PM at the Miners Foundry, 325 Spring St., where city staff will walk residents through the Deer Creek Hazardous Fuels Reduction Project: roughly 174 acres of mechanical treatment on the canyon's south side, scheduled from October through February. The city says the presentation will cover treatment areas, methods, timelines, and environmental protections, with time for questions.

The City Council follows Wednesday, October 28 at 6:30 PM at City Hall, 317 Broad St. The business agenda carries a Regional Public Safety Dispatch Services Agreement among Nevada County, Grass Valley, Truckee, and Nevada City; a resolution supporting Nevada Irrigation District's Proposition 4 dam safety grant application; a county presentation on reenvisioning western county transit; and a discussion of changing the council's start time and City Hall's public hours. On consent: a contract of up to $125,971 for the wastewater plant's SCADA controls, second readings of the water-efficient landscape and cannabis-tax ordinances, and SYRCL's fee-waiver and street-closure requests for the Wild & Scenic Film Festival. Written comment is due by 3:00 PM on meeting day. The full digest is in The Docket below, and Thursday's edition carries the meeting summary.

Grass Valley's council takes up its own version of the dispatch deal a day earlier. Its Tuesday agenda includes a five-year dispatch and 911 agreement with the county priced at $773,054 for fiscal 2026-27, rising 5 percent a year, with staff also asked to research the cost of a city-run dispatch center, The Union reports. Elections officials, meanwhile, advise anyone voting by mail to post the ballot by Tuesday, October 27; the Rood Center vote center is open daily, 9 AM to 5 PM.

Sources: City of Nevada City via YubaNet, Oct 21; October 28 agenda, City of Nevada City; The Union, Oct 24; Nevada County Elections via YubaNet

Election · Federal Demand

DOJ's 48-Hour Clock Runs Out; County Response Still Pending

The 48-hour deadline in the U.S. Justice Department's hand-delivered letter to the Nevada County Elections Office, which named 10 residents the department flags as likely non-citizens and demanded their ballots be set aside, lapses this weekend. As of YubaNet's last update Saturday afternoon, Registrar of Voters Armando Salud-Ambriz had said the office would review the list and respond, that it is following all applicable state and federal laws, and that a formal response was being prepared with county counsel and guidance from the Secretary of State. No county response had been published as of this morning.

The letters reached many California counties Friday. The Associated Press reported the demands cover roughly 20,000 voters statewide and requested sensitive information including Social Security numbers. Napa County's registrar said the letters aim to raise doubts about the election's integrity and that his office keeps counting; the state attorney general's office said Mendocino County received a list of people who did not live there; and Los Angeles County told a court that a preliminary review of 200 names found more than 40 percent had canceled registrations and nearly four in ten were born in the United States. The top federal prosecutor in Los Angeles warned that officials who knowingly process a non-citizen's ballot face "legal jeopardy."

Sources: YubaNet, updated Oct 24, 3:38 PM; Associated Press via ABC News, Oct 24

Conditions · Winter Ahead (updated 5 PM)

County Says Get Ready For An El Niño Winter; Rain Odds Rise For Late This Week

Nevada County officials are urging residents to prepare now for a possibly stormy winter, with forecasters watching a strong El Niño. County Fire Chief Mike Stewart and the Office of Emergency Services list the basics: sign up for Nevada County Alerts at ReadyNevadaCounty.org/EmergencyAlerts; clean gutters, trim limbs over roofs, and secure outdoor items; service woodstoves and practice generator safety; clear culverts on driveways and private roads; and keep a winter bag in the car and never drive through floodwater. The county provides free self-serve sand at sites listed at ReadyNevadaCounty.org/Sand; bring your own shovel and bags. Flooding, mudslides, or downed trees on county roads go to Public Works at (530) 265-1411; downed power lines go to 911 and PG&E.

The first test may come soon. National Weather Service Sacramento guidance relayed Saturday calls for dry weather through the weekend, isolated mountain showers north of Interstate 80 possible Monday through Thursday, temperatures slightly below normal and trending cooler, and increasing confidence in widespread precipitation late in the week, with timing and amounts still uncertain. Sunday morning's update, at 8:16 AM, holds highs slightly below normal into the week and cooler late in the week, adds breezy southwest winds Monday over the Sierra and southern Cascades with gusts of 30 to 40 mph, calls it mainly dry through midweek, and keeps good confidence in widespread precipitation by late week. PG&E's shutoff page, checked again this evening, still shows no event since October 17, and no red flag warning or new local fire appeared in YubaNet's log or fire page.

Sources: Nevada County via The Union, Oct 22; NWS Sacramento via YubaNet's Happening Now log, Oct 24 and Oct 25; PG&E newsroom; temperatures via OpenSnow, Oct 25.

Seven Hills District · Film

The Onyx To Join The Film Festival's Nonprofit

The Onyx Theatre is to become part of The Magic Lantern, the nonprofit behind the Nevada City Film Festival, by the end of 2026, keeping its management, staff, and programming. The festival was founded in 2001 at the Onyx's predecessor, the Magic Theatre, by Jeff Clark, who still owns the Onyx and said the combination "just made too much sense." Festival Executive Director Jesse Locks called it a step toward long-term sustainability for both.

Hey Good Lookin', Whatcha Got Cookin'

Hunter's Moon Squash and Sage Rigatoni (a Nugget original). Halve and seed a small butternut squash, cut it into half-inch cubes, toss with olive oil, salt, and a pinch of red pepper flakes, and roast at 425° for 25 minutes until the edges brown. Meanwhile boil a pound of rigatoni in well-salted water and save a cup of the cooking water before draining. In a wide skillet, melt four tablespoons of butter over medium heat with a dozen sage leaves until the butter turns nut-brown and the leaves crisp, then add two sliced cloves of garlic for half a minute. Toss in the pasta, the squash, a handful of grated Parmesan, and splashes of the pasta water until glossy, finish with black pepper and a squeeze of lemon, and scatter toasted walnuts on top. Serves four, or two and tomorrow's lunch, which on World Pasta Day counts as planning ahead. Readers' own recipes are welcome through the house channel and run with credit and permission.
