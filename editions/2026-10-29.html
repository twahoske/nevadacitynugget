Election · Five Days Out

County Finds No Non-Citizen Voting Among The 10 Flagged Names

Nevada County Elections said Wednesday that its review of the 10 voters the Justice Department named as likely non-citizens found no evidence of non-citizen voting in the November 3 election. Per the county, one name has no Nevada County registration record; seven had registrations canceled or made inactive before the demand letter arrived, were issued no ballot, and have no voting record; and two are currently registered and attested to U.S. citizenship under penalty of perjury on their latest registrations. Registrar of Voters Armando Salud-Ambriz said the public "can have confidence in the integrity of our election." The county's release does not say whether it sent the Justice Department the identification records its letter requested.

Ballots go in any official drop box, including the 24-hour box at Nevada City Hall, until 8 PM on Election Day, Tuesday, November 3, and the Rood Center vote center is open daily 9 AM to 5 PM through Monday. Nevada City's own question is Measure Q, the short-term rental permit measure. County Public Works continues pulling private signs, political ones included, from county road rights-of-way; owners can reclaim them within 60 days by appointment at (530) 265-1411.

The Nugget · Catching up on the August 26, 2026 City Council meeting

Council Puts Entheogens At The Bottom Of The Police To-Do List

Resolution 2026-29 passed unanimously · Covers personal, noncommercial use by adults 21 and over · Sales and businesses are expressly left out

At its regular meeting on Wednesday, August 26, 2026, the Nevada City Council unanimously adopted Resolution 2026-29, declaring certain adult personal, noncommercial conduct involving naturally occurring entheogenic plants and fungi among the city's lowest local law enforcement priorities, The Union reported two days later. The Nugget records the meeting here, two months on, at the publisher's request, because what the resolution does and does not do is likely to matter in the months ahead.

What it covers. According to the staff report as summarized in The Union's meeting preview, the policy applies to adults 21 and older and covers personal planting, cultivation, possession, and transportation of naturally occurring entheogenic plants and fungi, along with giving them away without payment. The preview named psilocybin, psilocyn, DMT, ibogaine, and mescaline. Investigating, citing, or arresting someone solely for that conduct becomes one of the city's lowest enforcement priorities. The policy binds only Nevada City's own public safety personnel, does not limit outside agencies or the courts, and leaves officer discretion in place. Per the staff report, Councilmember Doug Fleming and Vice Mayor Gary Petersen brought the item forward after requests from community members and local practitioners.

What it does not cover. Commercial activity is carved out: sales, paid delivery, paid administration, compensated services, and any conduct tied to a business arrangement. So are synthetic analogs, anything involving minors or schools and youth facilities, public use or intoxication, impaired driving, hazardous processing, and environmental harm. The resolution does not legalize or decriminalize anything barred by state or federal law, creates no immunity or legal defense, and does not authorize any commercial activity. Police Chief Dan Foss said at the meeting that these substances "remain illegal per state and federal law," according to The Union, and spoke of a chief's need to understand the values of the community the department serves.

Who spoke. The city's public comment file for the meeting holds seven written comments on the item, all in support, from residents and practitioners in Nevada City, Grass Valley, and beyond, most describing personal experience with depression, anxiety, trauma, or addiction. At the meeting, The Union reported, Nevada County Entheogenic Association CEO Shaun Robinson and Vice President Aaron Lane spoke in favor, and support also came from the Nevada County Cannabis Alliance and local health practitioners. The Nugget found no written comment opposing the resolution in the city's file.

Business and zoning items on the same agenda. The council also had on its consent agenda the second reading and adoption of Ordinance 2026-04, a cannabis business tax, and Ordinance 2026-05, governing cannabis dispensaries and other cannabis businesses and activities. The Nugget has not confirmed that vote: the meeting's action minutes went to the council for approval on September 9, but that packet could not be retrieved for this report. The cannabis tax returns on the October 28 agenda as Ordinance 2026-06, a second reading amending Section 5.06.050.

The rest of the evening, per the agenda: a closed-session performance evaluation of the City Manager; a proclamation for Penzance Twinning Day; consent items awarding a paving repair contract to PSCE (up to $75,020), a street striping contract to Traffic Limited (up to $32,825 a year, 2026 to 2029), and a Miners Foundry sound system contract to Cameron Professional Audio (up to $74,000); a request that NCTC allocate $115,768 in Local Transportation Funds for public transit; ratification of a $50,000 National Forest Foundation grant; approval of the Superior Court's Law Day event application; and a call for applicants to fill Fire Safety Advisory Committee vacancies. Outcomes on those items await the minutes.

The Nugget will keep following how the resolution plays out in town, including any business licenses, use permits, or zoning questions that reach City Hall.

Sparky Parker Goes Big On Broad Street

The Big Works show fills the brick wall at 316 Broad St. · Five decades of painting, now at full scale · Open Friday through Sunday, wine at hand

David "Sparky" Parker has wanted his paintings on the long brick wall of the Szabo Vineyards tasting room for years. Now they are there. His new exhibition, the Big Works show, hangs at the tasting room at 316 Broad Street, and Parker is plainly delighted with the fit. "I've wanted to show on this wall for years, and this work is just magic on it," he told the Nugget. "I am so excited about that."

The show's name is a fair warning. "They're all large pieces, so maybe they won't fit over your couch," Parker said. "It might be a little bigger than that." What fills the canvases, in his words, is "my use of dramatic color and a few splashes and lines and things like that." His verdict on the result: "I'm very pleased."

Asked what inspired the work, Parker pointed to a long career rather than a single spark. "I've been painting for five decades, so it's an evolution, and I decided that I hadn't painted big for a while and I needed it," he said. "I just needed to paint big."

If an artist learns from life experience, Parker came well supplied. He has been a forest firefighter, a recreation advocate and coach, a Sierra College trustee, and mayor of Nevada City, and he has founded numerous community initiatives, some serious and some just for fun, which makes him one of the more colorful characters in a town that does not lack for them. His invitation to readers is simple: come down, see the paintings, "and then have some wine!"

See it: Szabo Vineyards Tasting Room, 316 Broad Street, downtown Nevada City. Open Friday, Saturday, and Sunday, afternoons and early evenings. More on the artist at his Facebook page; the tasting room's page is at Szabo Vineyards.

The Onyx Joins The Film Festival's Nonprofit

By the end of 2026 the Onyx Theatre will be formally united with The Magic Lantern, the 501(c)(3) nonprofit that runs the Nevada City Film Festival, keeping its current management, staff, and programming, according to a submitted article in The Union. The Nevada Irrigation District board approved its 2027 budget Wednesday, with projected outflows of about $143.7 million, The Union reports; the vote count was not reported. The board also received the five-year Yuba/Bear River watershed sanitary survey, which found tap water safe to drink, recorded higher turbidity and E. coli in the lower watershed, and listed 59 violations for Nevada City over the study period, which the consultant said could pose a potential supply vulnerability; the article does not say what kind. The report goes to the State Water Board by December.

Source: The Union, Oct 29

Hey Good Lookin', Whatcha Got Cookin'

Steel-Cut Oats With Roasted Pears And Toasted Walnuts (a Nugget original). The night before, bring 4 cups of water and a pinch of salt to a boil, stir in 1 cup of steel-cut oats, boil one minute, cover, and leave it on the cold stove overnight. In the morning heat the oven to 400°, halve and core two ripe Bosc pears, rub them with a little butter and brown sugar, and roast cut side down about 20 minutes, until they slump and caramelize at the edges, while a handful of walnuts toasts on the same pan for the last five. Warm the oats with a splash of milk, a little cinnamon, and a spoonful of maple syrup, then top each bowl with a pear half, the walnuts, and the pan juices. Serves four, or two hermits observing the day properly. Readers' own recipes are welcome through the house channel and run with credit and permission.
