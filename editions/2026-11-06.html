City Hall & Politics

Election · Measure Q · (updated 5 PM)

The Count Resumes This Afternoon, With Measure Q Ahead

First post-election update due at 4:30 PM · 24,150 county ballots still to count · Measure Q led 602 to 378 at the end of election night · Mailed ballots have until Tuesday to arrive

Nevada County Elections posts its first results update since election night at 4:30 this afternoon, and Nevada City will learn whether the late ballots move Measure Q, the short-term rental measure. When counting stopped early Wednesday, the measure led 602 yes to 378 no, 61.4 percent to 38.6 percent, with all four city precincts reporting. A simple majority decides it. If it holds, short-term rentals inside city limits move from today's registration rules to a city permit with tighter eligibility and operating rules.

What is left. The county says 24,150 ballots remain countywide on top of the 29,155 counted election night: 22,000 received on or before Election Day, 450 received since, 50 forwarded from other counties, 50 provisional and 350 same-day-registration provisional ballots, and 1,250 damaged ballots or ballots needing review, with 304 left to cure. The figure was revised Thursday evening from 18,650 after the Elections office published a second unprocessed-ballots sheet. It gave no Nevada City breakdown, so how many of those ballots are the city's is not known. Ballots postmarked by Election Day still count if they reach the county by Tuesday, Nov. 10.

After today. Updates follow every Tuesday and Friday at 4:30 PM. The public 1 percent hand count is Thursday, Nov. 12, at 9 AM at the Ballot Processing Center, 12740 Loma Rica Drive, Grass Valley. Certified results come no earlier than Nov. 25; the county must certify by Dec. 3. The Nugget will report this afternoon's numbers in tomorrow's edition.

This evening. The 4:30 PM update could not be retrieved for this edition: the county's results page would not load for the Nugget, and no outlet had reported the new numbers by press time. KNCO, reporting Friday on the election-night count of about 61 percent yes, noted that the measure caps whole-home short-term rentals at 2 percent of the city's housing stock and ADU and junior ADU rentals at 1 percent, and requires 24-hour emergency contacts, occupancy and parking limits, and fire safety and defensible-space measures.

Sources: YubaNet, Nov. 5, updated 5:59 PM (ballots remaining); KNCO, Nov. 6 (measure terms); Nevada County Elections results page, final election-night count, 12:24 AM Nov. 4; Nevada County Elections, Nov. 2 (count schedule, hand count, certification); City Attorney's Impartial Analysis.

City Council · Schedule · (updated 5 PM)

Thursday's Council Agenda Is Out: Police Radios, A Pond Expansion, And The Trails Plan

The city posted the agenda for the council's only November session Friday afternoon. The meeting is Thursday, Nov. 12, at 6:30 PM at City Hall, 317 Broad St., moved a day for Veterans Day; the Nov. 25 meeting is cancelled. The consent calendar would accept a $350,000 federal COPS grant and buy Motorola public safety radios without bidding, extend the city's solid waste consultant for a year at up to $24,000, and send a letter backing the Greater Cement Hill Neighborhood Association's bid to buy the 2.2-acre Pello Property beside Hirschman's Pond. In business, the council would name the mayor and vice mayor to an ad hoc committee reviewing the Measure M and Measure C oversight committee, and hear a presentation on the Active Transportation, Trails, and Complete Streets Master Plan. The full item-by-item digest is in The Docket below.

Merchant of the Day

No. 5 · Bakery, Brewery & Restaurant · 211 Commercial Street

Three Forks Bakery & Brewing: Bread, Beer, And Pizza From The Same Wood Fire

Open since 2014 in a former furniture shop · More than 60 on staff · Emerald Pool IPA is the house favorite

Three Forks has done several jobs at once since it opened in 2014. Its website calls it a microbrewery, a full-service coffeeshop, a bakery, and a farm-to-table restaurant, and describes the place as a community hub "here for the love of people, food and our beautiful forest and river." It has never moved from 211 Commercial Street, a former furniture shop with a two-story loft that the owners renovated with building owner Gary Tintle, The Union reported when the business turned ten in 2024.

Made in house. Owner and general manager Hayley Evans told The Union that the kitchen makes everything it can on site: bread, condiments, and meats, down to butchering whole animals and curing its own bacon, with seasonal ingredients from local farmers and ranchers. The menu changes weekly with the produce, the bakery case daily. This season's posted menu runs to wood-fired sourdough pizzas such as house sausage with pickled jalapeño, and shiitake with kale pesto, plus soups, salads, and a lamb and white bean stew. The flagship beer is the Emerald Pool IPA, on tap, in bottles, and poured at other local accounts; the posted menu also lists a 13 percent imperial stout called Quoth the Raven. On Sundays, per The Union, the croissants are gone by 11.

Who comes in. Weekdays are mostly regulars, Evans told The Union; weekends add the Farmers Market crowd and visitors. The business employed more than 60 people at its tenth anniversary, and Evans put the priorities plainly: "We're here for the community and for our staff."

Pay a visit: 211 Commercial Street, Nevada City. 530-470-8333. threeforksnc.com. Counter service, no reservations. Posted winter hours: 8 AM to 9 PM Sunday, Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday; 8 AM to 10 PM Friday and Saturday; closed Tuesday.

Arts & Culture

Courtesy Notice · Promotional · 210 Main Street, upstairs over Treats

Twelve Artists Fill The Red Dot For A Winter Art Market

Nov. 6 through Dec. 20 at the Red Dot Gallery on Main · An artist-owned gallery above Treats · Open Thursday through Sunday, 1 to 5

Climb the stairs above Treats at 210 Main Street and you reach the Red Dot Gallery on Main, a small, artist-owned gallery that opened downtown this spring. From Nov. 6 through Dec. 20 the gallery will fill its walls with the work of 12 artists for a holiday Winter Art Market, according to the gallery's flyer.

Who's behind it. The gallery's three principal artists are Jerianne Van Dijk, known for watercolor worked with ink; Kathryn "Kathy" Wronski, who paints mostly in oil, makes colorful, whimsical prints, and answers to "the Dog Lady" for her dog portraits; and Peggy Wright, a former art educator and gallery owner who also owns Treats downstairs. The Union reported in April that the artists mind the gallery themselves in rotation and keep a corner workspace where visitors can watch a painter at work. Van Dijk told the paper she likes to "smell the color."

About that name. In the art world, a red dot beside a painting means it has sold, and the artists told The Union they picked the name out of optimism. The paper also described a vending machine, Wright's idea, that drops a tiny 3-by-3-inch painting by one of the artists, chosen at random.

Pay a visit: Red Dot Gallery on Main, 210 Main Street, Nevada City, upstairs over Treats; enter through the ice cream shop. Thursday through Sunday, 1 to 5 p.m. The flyer adds Victorian Christmas hours during the market: Wednesdays 5 to 9 p.m. and Sundays 1 to 5 p.m. Hours are as the gallery posts them; check before you go.

Hey Good Lookin', Whatcha Got Cookin'

Gold Country Harvest Nachos With Roasted Squash And Black Beans (a Nugget original). Heat the oven to 425. Peel and cube half a small butternut squash into half-inch pieces, toss them with a tablespoon of olive oil, a teaspoon of smoked paprika, half a teaspoon of cumin, and a good pinch of salt, and roast on a sheet pan for 20 minutes, turning once, until the edges brown. Meanwhile warm a drained can of black beans in a small pan with a minced clove of garlic, a squeeze of lime, and a splash of water, mashing about a third of them so they cling. Spread a big bag of sturdy tortilla chips on a second sheet pan, spoon over the beans and squash, and scatter on two cups of grated sharp cheddar and Monterey Jack, half and half, and a few pickled jalapeño rings. Bake 8 to 10 minutes, until the cheese runs. Finish with chopped cilantro, thinly sliced red onion, a crumble of cotija, and sour cream thinned with lime juice. Serves four for supper, or one Saturday afternoon and the better part of a ballgame.
