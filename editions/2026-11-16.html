Weather · Tuesday Night Through Thursday (updated 5 PM)

One Dry Day, Then The Wettest Storm Of The Month

Showers taper off today · Frost possible Tuesday morning · Up to 2 inches of rain in the foothills Tuesday night to Friday · Wind Advisory, gusts to 50 mph · Winter Storm Warning for the Sierra above 4,000 feet

The weekend's storm winds down today, and the weather service says the next one will be bigger. In its Sunday afternoon discussion, the National Weather Service in Sacramento said "confidence is now high" in a colder, wetter system arriving Tuesday night and lasting into Friday morning. It forecast 1.5 to 2.75 inches of rain in the foothills, 1.25 to 2 inches in the Valley, and 2 to 3.35 inches over the mountains, with strong southerly winds gusting 35 to 45 mph across the Valley, Delta, and foothills from late Tuesday night through Thursday. A forecast the office issued Sunday morning, relayed by YubaNet, had put the foothills at 2 to 4 inches.

Monday afternoon's update. The weather service's 1:08 PM discussion turned the watch into a Winter Storm Warning for the northern Sierra and southern Cascades above 4,000 feet, 10 PM Tuesday to 4 AM Friday, now calling for 1 to 3 feet of snow above 5,000 feet, locally up to 4 feet on the highest peaks, up to a foot between 4,000 and 5,000 feet, and a dusting possible as low as 3,500 feet. It also issued a Wind Advisory for Valley, Delta and foothill zones from 10 PM Tuesday to 4 PM Wednesday, with gusts up to 50 mph, and now puts rain at up to 2 inches in the Valley and foothills from Tuesday evening through early Friday, more in higher terrain. Isolated thunderstorms are possible this afternoon and evening, mainly near Yuba City, and again Wednesday and Thursday afternoons; Thursday is the likeliest, with a low chance of a funnel cloud in the stronger storms. The weather service's hazards page for western Nevada County's zone listed no active product when the Nugget checked at 5 PM. PG&E's newsroom still shows no power shutoff activity.

Snow, and how low. Snow levels start around 3,500 to 4,500 feet Tuesday night, well above Nevada City at 2,477 feet, then rise to 4,500 to 6,000 feet by late Wednesday morning. The weather service expects 1 to 2 feet above 5,000 feet, up to 3 feet on the highest peaks, and 4 to 8 inches between 4,000 and 5,000 feet, heaviest Wednesday afternoon and evening. This morning's Winter Storm Watch for the northern Sierra and southern Cascades above 4,000 feet has since become a warning (see above), and mountain travel is expected to be very difficult. There is a 10 to 20 percent chance of thunderstorms Wednesday and Thursday, and the discussion warns of ponding on roads and rising small streams.

Today and Tuesday. Showers linger into this evening, and a Winter Weather Advisory runs until 10 PM tonight for the Sierra above 5,000 to 6,000 feet, so expect slick passes on the evening commute. OpenSnow puts Nevada City's high at 46 today. Tuesday is the break: dry, with cold mornings and possible frost in the foothills. As of this morning the weather service lists no watch, warning, or advisory for western Nevada County itself, and PG&E's newsroom shows no power shutoff activity.

Before Tuesday night. Clear gutters and drains while it is dry. The county's free self-serve sand is at Highway 49 and East Broad Street (bring a shovel and bags), and the county holds a free sand and winter preparedness event at its warehouse there Wednesday, 1:30 to 4 PM. The weather service says the pattern may stay active into Thanksgiving week.

Sources: NWS Sacramento, Area Forecast Discussion, 2:01 PM Nov. 15, and 1:08 PM Nov. 16; YubaNet Happening Now, Nov. 16; the weather service's hazards page for western Nevada County, checked this morning and at 5 PM; NWS Sacramento via YubaNet, Nov. 15; OpenSnow, Nevada City; PG&E newsroom; Nevada County, Nov. 11.

City Hall & Politics

Evening Update · Wildfire Preparedness

Fire Safe Council Of Nevada County Files For Chapter 7 Bankruptcy

The Fire Safe Council of Nevada County, which closed its doors in August, is filing for Chapter 7 bankruptcy, its board of directors announced this afternoon. Board Chair Pete Williams said the step came "after struggling for several years to find ways to pay the bills" and was "the only realistic option." "This decision is deeply painful for us on the board," he said. A court-appointed trustee will oversee the case, and money from selling the organization's assets will go to its creditors; the board said the process is not expected to finish this year. The council ceases operations after 28 years. Board volunteers say they will keep sharing information on defensible space and Firewise Communities, and requests for a Defensible Space Advisory Visit or help with a Firewise Community go to firesafehelp@gmail.com. The announcement does not say how much the organization owes or what its assets are.

Source: Fire Safe Council of Nevada County via YubaNet, Nov. 16.

Evening Update · Deer Creek Canyon & City Open Space

City Crews Begin Burning Brush Piles Around Deer Creek

Nevada City's fuels crew began burning brush piles this morning on two city wildfire projects, the Deer Creek Hazardous Fuels Reduction Project and the Nevada City Open Space Fuels Reduction Project, YubaNet's log reported at 9:54 AM. Per the city Office of Emergency Services, burning continues through mid-December as weather and air quality allow, generally 8 AM to 5 PM, in Deer Creek Canyon and on city-owned open space, including piles in the Tribulation Trail area near Champion Mine Road and west of Providence Mine Road; a project sign shown with the announcement lists tentative dates of Nov. 16 to Dec. 4. The piles are debris from earlier thinning, and the Deer Creek project covers about 350 acres on the south side of the canyon. The work is paid for by Measure C, the wildfire prevention sales tax city voters approved in 2024. "The City will monitor air quality and take appropriate measures to reduce smoke impacts to the downtown area," the announcement says. Expect some smoke, crews, and temporary signs near the sites. Questions go to Nevada City OES, (530) 265-2496 ext. 121, or nevadacityca.gov/wildfirepreparedness.

Sources: City of Nevada City via YubaNet; YubaNet Happening Now, 9:54 AM Nov. 16.

Evening Update · Real Estate

Fewest October Home Sales In A Decade

Western Nevada County recorded 84 home sales in October, down from 113 a year earlier and the fewest for the month in at least a decade, according to a market report by Grass Valley Realtor Brian Hamilton. The median sale price was $542,500, against $550,000 a year ago; homes sold in a median 20 days on market, and 368 were listed at month's end. The report does not break out Nevada City.

Arts & Culture · Looking Ahead

Wild & Scenic Turns 25: The Film Festival Comes Back Feb. 18 to 22

The 25th annual festival picks "Momentum" as its theme · Early-bird passes on sale · The art call closes Nov. 30

SYRCL's Wild & Scenic Film Festival, the environmental film festival that fills downtown Nevada City and Grass Valley every winter, has set its 25th annual edition for Thursday, Feb. 18, through Monday, Feb. 22, 2027, according to the festival's website. For the anniversary it has chosen "Momentum" as its guiding theme.

The festival. SYRCL, the South Yuba River Citizens League, was founded in 1983 by activists working to protect the South Yuba from dams, and it produces the festival. The festival's own page describes a flagship event of more than 100 films, held each year in Nevada City and Grass Valley, before Wild & Scenic goes on tour to more than 125 locations. The 2027 films and venues have not been posted yet; the Nugget will run them when they are.

Passes. Early-bird passes are on sale now, and the festival is marking the 25th with "$25 off." Buy passes from the festival's ticket page.

Artists, last call. The festival's art exhibition, run with Nevada County Arts, takes submissions until a final deadline of Nov. 30: up to three pieces, all for sale, in 2-D, 3-D, or photography. Chosen work hangs in the Granucci Gallery at The Center for the Arts in Grass Valley, Feb. 18 to March 14, 2027, and at the Nevada County Government Center in Nevada City, Jan. 18 to April 30, 2027. Details and submissions; questions to art@wildandscenicfilmfestival.org or 530-264-7031.

Got a spare room? The festival is looking for locals to host a filmmaker or special guest during the festival, and for volunteers. Lodging host questions go to the festival office, (530) 265-5961, ext. 232.

The Nugget runs this as a community notice; no one paid for it, and the paper has no ties to the festival. Hey Good Lookin', Whatcha Got Cookin'

Storm-Week White Beans With Greens and a Parmesan Rind (a Nugget original). Soak a pound of dried cannellini overnight, or skip ahead with three drained cans. In a heavy pot, warm a good glug of olive oil and cook a chopped onion, two diced carrots, and two sliced celery ribs with a pinch of salt until soft, about ten minutes; add four smashed garlic cloves, a teaspoon of chopped rosemary, and a pinch of red pepper flakes for a minute more. Add the beans, two quarts of chicken or vegetable stock, a bay leaf, and the rind you have been saving from the end of a wedge of Parmesan, and simmer, partly covered, until the soaked beans are creamy, about an hour (twenty minutes for canned). Mash a cup of beans against the side of the pot to thicken it, then stir in a big bunch of chopped kale or chard and cook until tender. Fish out the bay leaf and what is left of the rind, finish with the juice of half a lemon, salt and pepper to taste, and a swirl of oil in each bowl, with toasted bread alongside. It keeps for days, which is the point of making it before Tuesday night.
