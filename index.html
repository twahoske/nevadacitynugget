Election · Measure Q · Next Count Wednesday

Measure Q Waits On Wednesday's Count, Still At 60 Percent

Measure Q, Nevada City's short-term rental permit measure, stands at 967 Yes (59.99 percent) to 645 No (40.01 percent) with all four city precincts reporting, unchanged since Friday's update, according to Nevada County Elections figures posted by YubaNet.

What is left. Countywide, about 850 ballots were still to be processed and 273 were waiting on voters to cure signature or other problems after Friday's count. The county does not break the remaining ballots out by city, and its results page does not mark the measure as decided.

Next. The next update is set for Wednesday, Nov. 25, in the late afternoon. Every figure is unofficial until the county certifies; the state deadline is Dec. 3. Down the hill, Grass Valley's council race shows Scott Beesley (35.41 percent) and Bob Branstrom (33.69 percent) leading for the two seats, with no winners marked.

Sources: Nevada County Elections via YubaNet, "Election Results Update Nov. 20"; Nevada County, Nov. 3 election results.

County · Animal Services · Comment Through Nov. 30

Six Days Left To Comment On The New County Animal Shelter

Written comments on the environmental document for Nevada County's planned animal shelter at 12270 La Barr Meadows Road, south of Grass Valley, are due by 5 PM Monday, Nov. 30. The project is estimated at about $17 million, and the county has asked Nevada City and Grass Valley, which share the aging Freeman Lane shelter, to join a regional facility, The Union reported.

The Mitigated Negative Declaration flags potentially significant impacts on biological, cultural and tribal cultural resources, noise, and utilities. Comments go to Assistant Planner Aleena Church, county Planning Department, 950 Maidu Ave., Suite 170, Nevada City. The county Zoning Administrator takes final action after a hearing not yet scheduled.

Source: The Union, Nov. 19.

County · Civic Education · Apply By Jan. 8

The County Opens Its Citizen's Academy To Applicants

Nevada County is taking applications through Jan. 8, 2027, for its Citizen's Academy, a series that takes residents behind the scenes of county government. Applications are at nevadacountyca.gov/citizensacademy.

Read the whole story

Participants tour eight county facilities and hear more than twenty department presentations, from agriculture to zoning, in Tuesday evening sessions, the county said in its Nov. 23 announcement. "Local government touches nearly every aspect of our daily lives," said County Executive Officer Alison Lehman. Past participants quoted in the release include Nevada County Superintendent of Schools John Baggett.

Source: Nevada County, Nov. 23.

County · Code Enforcement · Comment Through Dec. 16

Comment Continues On A County Public Nuisance Ordinance

Nevada County is taking comment through Dec. 16 on a proposed Public Nuisance Abatement Ordinance, a new Chapter 7.02 of the county code. It would apply in the unincorporated county, not inside Nevada City.

Read the whole story

The draft defines nuisances such as unsafe structures, fire hazards, illegal dumping, and recurring noise or traffic problems; the county says it creates no new zoning or building standards and would seek voluntary compliance first. A first reading before the Board of Supervisors is expected in January 2027. Comments go to CodeCompliance@nevadacountyca.gov with "Public Nuisance Abatement Ordinance" in the subject line.

Source: Nevada County via The Union, Nov. 19.

Weather · Thanksgiving Week

The Midweek Storm Fizzles, And Thanksgiving Looks Dry

A stray shower or thunderstorm possible this afternoon into Wednesday · Dry and mild Thanksgiving · Warmer, unsettled weather returns for the weekend

The storm that was supposed to soak Thanksgiving week has, in the weather service's words, "trended much drier." NWS Sacramento's Monday update still allows for scattered rain, light mountain snow above 6,000 feet, breezy winds and isolated thunderstorms from this afternoon into Wednesday, but rates the weather risk minor to none.

Thanksgiving and after. The forecast is dry for the holiday everywhere but western Shasta County, with possible dense fog in the valleys Thursday morning, and mostly dry with seasonable afternoons Friday. Unsettled weather returns over the weekend, trending warmer, with snow levels above 7,000 feet; the weather service says the details remain uncertain.

Temperatures. OpenSnow's Nevada City forecast, last read Monday morning, has 54 and 41 today, 58 Wednesday, 63 on Thanksgiving, and 66 Friday, cooling to the low 50s by Sunday.

Travel. Caltrans has a short work week on Interstate 80: overnight lane closures between Magra and Gold Run and on several Placer County stretches end at 5 AM Wednesday, while the Crystal Springs and Drum Forebay ramps and the westbound Yuba Gap off-ramp stay closed until further notice. Check Caltrans QuickMap or 511 before heading over the summit.

Fire and power. No active wildfires are listed for the county or nearby foothills, PG&E's shutoff page lists no planned power shutoff, and burning remains prohibited inside Nevada City limits.

Sources: NWS Sacramento via YubaNet, Nov. 23; YubaNet Happening Now; OpenSnow, Nevada City; Caltrans via YubaNet, Nov. 22; YubaNet Fire News; PG&E newsroom, checked this morning.

Hey Good Lookin', Whatcha Got Cookin'

Sardine Toasts With Fuyu Persimmon, Lemon And Toasted Walnuts (a Nugget original). Toast four thick slices of sourdough until crisp at the edges and rub each one lightly with the cut side of a garlic clove. Peel and thinly slice one firm, ripe Fuyu persimmon, the squat kind you can eat like an apple, and toss the slices with a squeeze of lemon, a pinch of salt and a few leaves of chopped parsley. Toast a small handful of walnuts in a dry skillet for three or four minutes, shaking the pan, then chop them roughly. Drain a tin of good olive-oil sardines, lay two or three fish on each toast, and press them gently with a fork so they spread. Fan the persimmon over the top, scatter on the walnuts, finish with a little grated lemon zest, a drizzle of the oil from the tin and a few grinds of black pepper. It makes a fast supper for two or an appetizer for four, and it is the one Thanksgiving-week dish nobody will ask you to bring again.
