City Hall & Politics

Election · Measure Q · Certified Nov. 25

Measure Q Passes With 60 Percent As The County Certifies The Vote

Final count 994 Yes to 659 No · A permit system replaces the city's rental registration rules · County turnout 71 percent · Council next meets Dec. 10

Nevada City voters have approved Measure Q, the city's residential short-term rental measure, by a final count of 994 Yes (60.13 percent) to 659 No (39.87 percent). Nevada County Registrar of Voters Armando Salud-Ambriz certified the results of the Nov. 3 General Election late Wednesday afternoon, calling them the final, official results as of 4:26 PM, Nov. 25.

What it does. As the county's results page describes it, the measure replaces the city's existing short-term rental registration rules with a new permitting system. The final tally moved little from the Nov. 20 update, when the measure led 967 to 645.

The numbers. 1,653 of the city's 2,424 registered voters cast a vote on the measure, with all four city precincts reporting and 166 ballots leaving it blank. Countywide, 54,485 ballots were cast, 71 percent of 76,687 registered voters, according to the county.

What comes next. The Nugget found no city announcement this morning on when the new permit rules take effect. City Hall's Agenda Center shows no new council agenda; the council next meets Thursday, Dec. 10, at 6 PM, a half hour earlier than usual, and the Planning Commission on Thursday, Dec. 17, at 1:30 PM.

Sources: Nevada County Elections via YubaNet, "Nevada County's November 2026 General Election Results Certified," Nov. 25; YubaNet, Nov. 20 update; City Agenda Center, checked this morning.

County · Animal Services · Comments Due Monday

Four Days Left To Comment On The New County Animal Shelter

Written comments on the environmental document for Nevada County's planned animal shelter at 12270 La Barr Meadows Road, south of Grass Valley, are due by 5 PM Monday, Nov. 30. The project is estimated at about $17 million, and the county has asked Nevada City and Grass Valley, which share the aging Freeman Lane shelter, to join a regional facility, The Union reported.

The Mitigated Negative Declaration flags potentially significant impacts on biological, cultural and tribal cultural resources, noise, and utilities. Comments go to Assistant Planner Aleena Church, county Planning Department, 950 Maidu Ave., Suite 170, Nevada City. The county Zoning Administrator takes final action after a hearing not yet scheduled.

Source: The Union, Nov. 19.

County · Code Enforcement · Comment Through Dec. 16

Comment Continues On A County Public Nuisance Ordinance

Nevada County is taking comment through Dec. 16 on a proposed Public Nuisance Abatement Ordinance, a new Chapter 7.02 of the county code, which would apply in the unincorporated county, not inside Nevada City. The Union reports that Penn Valley's Municipal Advisory Council supports the county's approach.

Read the whole story

The draft defines nuisances such as unsafe structures, fire hazards, illegal dumping, and recurring noise or traffic problems; the county says it creates no new zoning or building standards and would seek voluntary compliance first. A first reading before the Board of Supervisors is expected in January 2027. Comments go to CodeCompliance@nevadacountyca.gov with "Public Nuisance Abatement Ordinance" in the subject line. The Nugget could read only the headline of The Union's Penn Valley story this morning.

Weather · Holiday Weekend

Dry Through Saturday, Then A Warm, Wet Storm

Clear, mild Thanksgiving in the mid-60s · Morning fog possible · Rain arrives late Saturday night and lasts into Monday · Better than even odds of an inch or more in the foothills

The holiday stays dry. The National Weather Service in Sacramento, in a note relayed by YubaNet Wednesday morning, called for dry weather through Saturday with morning fog, then a "relatively warm, wet storm" from late Saturday night through Monday. Its map gave the northern Sierra foothills a 70 to 78 percent chance of more than an inch of rain from Sunday into Monday.

Temperatures. OpenSnow's Nevada City forecast has 64 and 47 today, 64 and 50 Friday and Saturday, then 53 Sunday, 50 Monday and 49 Tuesday.

Before the rain. A county-sponsored winter preparedness piece in The Union this week urges residents to sign up for Nevada County Alerts, protect pipes, plan safe backup power and service generators and stovepipes, charge phones before storms, and keep chains, blankets and water in the car. Resources are at ReadyNevadaCounty.org; Public Works is (530) 265-1411, PG&E 1-800-743-5000, and downed lines are a 911 call.

Shelter. Sierra Roots opens its emergency weather shelter at the Veterans Hall, 415 N. Pine St., when the forecast meets its triggers, among them four or more consecutive days of rain, a flood watch or warning for Nevada City or Grass Valley, or three straight overnight hours at 32 degrees or colder. It last opened Nov. 20 to 22. Information: 2-1-1, or the county HOME Team at (530) 470-2686.

Fire and power. YubaNet's fire page lists no active wildfire in the county or nearby foothills, and PG&E's shutoff page shows no event since Oct. 17. The planned pile burn off Augustine and New Rome roads northwest of town, which began Tuesday for about five days, may still send up smoke. Burning stays prohibited inside Nevada City limits.

Remembrance

In Memoriam · San Juan Ridge · KVMR 89.5

Roo Cantada, Who Built A Library On The Ridge And Rocked KVMR After Dark

Librarian, poet, festival maker and a voice on community radio for 25 years

Lisa "Roo" Cantada, the librarian who gave the San Juan Ridge its public library and a longtime voice on KVMR, died in mid-November, according to friends and fellow KVMR programmers who shared the news this month. Her family has not released details, and the Nugget will add them here if they do.

For more than a quarter century, if you wanted a book, a poem or a surf-rock deep cut on the San Juan Ridge, the road led to Roo Cantada. Her KVMR bio says she left Midwestern suburbia to build a life around "the word," spoken, written or sung, and she did exactly that, mostly for free and mostly for her neighbors. She came west from Michigan in the 1990s. Her friend Sam, a fellow broadcaster who met her then, remembers "a tiny girl with a large spirit."

The library she willed into being. Roo became the Twin Ridges school district's librarian in 1999, The Union reported, and in the early 2000s she opened the Grizzly Hill school library to the whole Ridge after hours. That community library ran for more than eight years and grew past 3,000 books on fundraiser money, according to the library's own history. When the school board shut the program down in 2011, she kept going: she teamed up with longtime librarian Mary Moore, found space through the Oak Tree Park and Recreation District, and helped form a nonprofit. The San Juan Ridge Community Library opened its doors at Oak Tree Park on June 8, 2014, staffed entirely by volunteers for its first years.

She worked unpaid through those years and, The Union reported, sold burritos to keep the lights on. Money from the county's Measure A, passed in 2016, finally gave the library a budget, and COVID-era grants let it open five days a week. Today it holds more than 11,000 titles, nearly all donated by neighbors, and serves the Ridge along with Camptonville, Alleghany and Pike. Every service is free. "I'm going to keep the doors open," she told The Union in 2024, and she did.

This fall the Community Foundation of Nevada County described her handling HR, fundraising, events and purchasing with a single part-time helper, while studying for a master's in library science after 27 years on the job, with a capstone aimed at designing a bigger library for the Ridge. Under her watch the place hosted ukulele, guitar, Spanish, weaving, qi gong, karate and craft classes, a Dr. Seuss birthday party, movie nights, Scary Story Walks, a dog parade and a monthly health clinic.

The poet and the producer. She founded the Nevada County Poetry Series in 2000, bringing Gary Snyder, Michael McClure and Diane di Prima to local audiences, and started Ridgestock, a homegrown music and sustainability festival on the Ridge. She produced it for 20 years before stepping away after the 2019 edition, and new volunteers revived it in 2023. She also lent a hand to the High Sierra Music Festival, Sam recalled, and many of the concerts she produced raised money for libraries and parks. She also served as a commissioner of the Oak Tree Park and Recreation District.

On the air. Roo joined KVMR in 2000, first alongside Joseph Guida and later T.E. Wolf. Over the years she produced Radio Poets Society and South of the Border, subbed widely on world music shows, and most recently hosted Rock Outside the Box, Tuesday nights from 10 to midnight, a mix the station described as electric surf, rock, pop and "general musical mayhem." Her bio called her daughter, Sophia, her best creative work.

From the publisher. When I signed off The Funk Hole, Roo took over my KVMR timeslot, and Tuesday nights only got stranger and better. I knew her as a fellow programmer at the station; the Nugget has no other tie to her or to the library. Rock on, Roo. Todd Wahoske

Photos from Roo Cantada's Instagram, @roorocknroll, run in her memory. Any service or memorial details the family shares will appear here. Corrections: news@nevadacitynugget.com.

Hey Good Lookin', Whatcha Got Cookin'

Day-After Turkey And Wild Rice Soup With Sage And Lemon (a Nugget original). Pull the last of the meat off tonight's carcass and set it aside, then put the bones in a big pot with an onion cut in half, a carrot, a celery rib, a bay leaf and enough cold water to cover by two inches; bring it just to a simmer and let it go, uncovered and never boiling, for two to three hours, then strain it, which leaves you about two quarts of stock and a kitchen that smells like the holiday all over again. In the cleaned pot melt two tablespoons of butter over medium heat and soften a diced onion, two diced carrots, two sliced celery ribs and two minced cloves of garlic with a pinch of salt for about eight minutes. Stir in three-quarters of a cup of rinsed wild rice, six chopped fresh sage leaves and a sprig of thyme, pour in the stock, and simmer, covered, for 45 minutes, until the rice splits and turns tender. Add two to three cups of the shredded turkey and a big handful of chopped kale, simmer five minutes more, then finish with the juice of half a lemon, plenty of black pepper and salt to taste; a spoonful of leftover gravy stirred in does no harm. It makes about six bowls, tastes better on Sunday when the rain comes in, and asks nothing more of anyone who has already cooked a turkey this week.
