What It MeansThe night in plain English

Bottom lineDemocrats have won both the U.S. Senate and the House, the AP says, and will run Congress come January. Here at home, Measure Q is passing comfortably in the county's count, though many local ballots are still to be counted.

  1. The SenateDemocrats win

    The AP has called the Senate for Democrats, who hold 53 of the 100 seats so far after Troy Jackson unseated Susan Collins in Maine. From January they will set the Senate's agenda, chair its committees and decide which of the president's nominees, from judges to Cabinet picks, get a vote.

  2. The HouseDemocrats win

    The AP called the House for Democrats at 10:24 PM; they have 220 seats so far, past the 218 needed, to the Republicans' 129. With both chambers, Democrats will control what bills reach the president's desk for the last two years of his term, and can open investigations of the administration.

  3. CaliforniaEarly count

    Xavier Becerra leads the race for governor with 65 percent of the statewide count, half the vote in; the new governor takes office in January. Prop. 40, the one-time tax on billionaires, is trailing, and voters have turned down voter ID at the polls (Prop. 39).

  4. Close to homeFirst count in

    Measure Q leads 64 to 36 percent in Nevada City. If it holds, short-term rentals in town move from a simple registration to a city permit with tighter rules. In the county's count, Bera leads Tucker 60 to 40 and James leads Hadwick 57 to 43, but those districts stretch well past the county. Mail ballots postmarked by today still count if they arrive by Nov. 10.

City Hall & Politics

Election · Today

Election Day: Polls Open 7 AM To 8 PM, And Measure Q Is The City's Question

Nine vote centers countywide, 7 AM to 8 PM · Drop box at City Hall until 8 PM · First returns shortly after 8 PM · Certified results no sooner than Nov. 25

Nevada County votes today. The county elections office says nine vote centers are open from 7 AM to 8 PM, with every location and its hours listed at NevadaCountyCa.gov/votehere. Ballots can also go in an official drop box, including the 24-hour box at City Hall, 317 Broad St., until 8 PM. A ballot sent by mail counts if it is postmarked today and arrives by November 10, but the county has warned that postmarks are applied in Sacramento and Reno, so a drop box is the surer route on the last day. Nevada City ballots carry Measure Q, the Residential Short-Term Rental Measure. The Weather Service expects rain showers through the day, tapering off this evening, so voters may want an umbrella in line.

When the numbers come. The county said Monday that the first results will post shortly after polls close at 8 PM, covering in-person votes and the mail ballots received and processed before today, with updates about every two hours until the in-person votes are counted. Mail ballots returned yesterday or today will not be in the first count. After election night, updates post every Friday and Tuesday at 4:30 PM at NevadaCountyCA.gov/November3Election. As of Monday morning, about 36 percent of the county's roughly 76,700 registered voters had cast ballots, the county said.

The slow part. Signatures on mail ballots must be checked, voters with missing or mismatched signatures given a chance to fix them, and provisional and same-day registration ballots reviewed. A public hand count of at least 1 percent of precincts is set for Thursday, November 12, at 9 AM at the Ballot Processing Center, 12740 Loma Rica Drive, Grass Valley. Certified results will come no earlier than 5 PM on November 25; state law sets a December 3 deadline. The county also noted a new state law that could hold back statewide election-night results if voting hours are extended anywhere because of a disruption; local results stay on schedule unless that happens at a Nevada County polling place. Election processing can be watched live on the Nevada County Elections YouTube channel. The Nugget will report Nevada City's returns, Measure Q first, in Wednesday's edition.

Sources: Nevada County Elections, Nov 2 (also via YubaNet); Nevada County Elections, Oct 31; Nevada County Elections, Oct 21 (postmark warning); Notice of Measure Q; NWS Sacramento via YubaNet's Happening Now log, Nov 2, 6:22 AM.

Fire · Burn Permits (updated 5 PM)

Burn Permits Back In Force As Of 8 AM Today

CAL FIRE's Nevada-Yuba-Placer Unit lifts its burn permit suspension for Nevada, Yuba, Placer, and Sierra counties at 8 AM today. Holders of current agriculture and residential permits may burn again on permissible burn days; agriculture burns still need a CAL FIRE inspection until peak fire season ends. Check for a burn day with the Northern Sierra Air Quality Management District, (530) 274-7928, before lighting. The rules: dry natural vegetation only, piles no larger than four feet across and four feet high, a 10-foot circle cleared to bare soil, water and a shovel at hand, an adult present throughout, and no burning on windy days. YubaNet's fires page lists no active incident in Nevada County or the nearby foothills this morning, and PG&E has announced no new power shutoff since its October 17 final update.

Evening update: Tuesday was declared a burn day in Nevada and Sierra counties, with CAL FIRE permits required and a suggested burn window of 9 AM to 3 PM, per YubaNet's 8:45 AM log entry, which adds that open burning is not allowed inside the city limits of Grass Valley or Nevada City. Merchant of the Day

No. 2 · Groceries · 735 Zion Street, Seven Hills District

SPD Market: Three Partners, One Family, Sixty-Seven Years On Zion Street

The letters stand for See, Painter, and Dilley · The Painters have run it alone since 1969 · A third generation now works the floor

What does SPD stand for? The Union has the answer: See, Painter, and Dilley, the three men who opened the market in October 1959. Bert See ran an auto parts store and Joe Dilley was a butcher. Lawrence Painter had already been in the grocery trade on Broad Street since 1951, and the partners left downtown for a new 12,000-square-foot store at Zion Street and Argall Way, in what is now the Seven Hills Business District. The store doubled in size within three years.

How it became a family store. In 1964 Painter and See bought out Dilley, and in 1969 Painter bought out See, The Union reported in 2009; the Painter family has owned the business ever since. A second store opened off McKnight Way in Grass Valley in the early 1980s. Lawrence and Carol Painter's four sons, David, Dennis, Doug, and Dean, took over the company in the early 1990s, and by 2014 Dennis's son Ben was managing the Nevada City store and its more than 30,000 products. The Union traced the family's Nevada County roots back to the 1880s.

What's on the shelves. SPD's website lists locally grown and organic produce, meat and seafood including products from local farmers, a wine and liquor selection that includes local growers, gluten-free and natural foods, and full-service catering. The Nevada City store also keeps the sort of counter a neighborhood relies on: a postal center, bill pay, money orders, and fishing licenses. Shoppers who sign up for SPD's Community Card send 3 percent of qualifying purchases to a school or nonprofit of their choosing. "We try to carry as much local product as we can," Ben Painter told The Union in 2014.

Pay a visit: 735 Zion Street, Nevada City, in the Seven Hills Business District. Store office 530-265-4596. spdmarket.com. SPD's second store is at 129 W. McKnight Way, Grass Valley.

Arts & Culture

Only in the Nugget

By Todd Wahoske · The Publisher's Interview

Sparky Parker Goes Big On Broad Street

The Big Works show fills the brick wall at 316 Broad St. · Five decades of painting, now at full scale · Open Friday through Sunday, wine at hand

David "Sparky" Parker has wanted his paintings on the long brick wall of the Szabo Vineyards tasting room for years. Now they are there. His new exhibition, the Big Works show, hangs at the tasting room at 316 Broad Street, and Parker is plainly delighted with the fit. "I've wanted to show on this wall for years, and this work is just magic on it," he told the Nugget. "I am so excited about that."

The show's name is a fair warning. "They're all large pieces, so maybe they won't fit over your couch," Parker said. "It might be a little bigger than that." What fills the canvases, in his words, is "my use of dramatic color and a few splashes and lines and things like that." His verdict on the result: "I'm very pleased."

Asked what inspired the work, Parker pointed to a long career rather than a single spark. "I've been painting for five decades, so it's an evolution, and I decided that I hadn't painted big for a while and I needed it," he said. "I just needed to paint big."

If an artist learns from life experience, Parker came well supplied. He has been a forest firefighter, a recreation advocate and coach, a Sierra College trustee, and mayor of Nevada City, and he has founded numerous community initiatives, some serious and some just for fun, which makes him one of the more colorful characters in a town that does not lack for them. His invitation to readers is simple: come down, see the paintings, "and then have some wine!"

See it: Szabo Vineyards Tasting Room, 316 Broad Street, downtown Nevada City. Open Friday, Saturday, and Sunday, afternoons and early evenings. More on the artist at his Facebook page; the tasting room's page is at Szabo Vineyards.

Hey Good Lookin', Whatcha Got Cookin'

Polling Place Grilled Cheese With Pear, Sharp Cheddar, And Rosemary Honey (a Nugget original). Warm two tablespoons of honey in a small pan with a sprig of rosemary and a pinch of chili flakes, then pull it off the heat to steep while you build. Butter the outsides of two slices of country sourdough, and on the unbuttered side of one lay thin slices of a ripe Bosc pear, a generous handful of grated sharp white cheddar, and a few leaves of baby arugula, finishing with more cheddar so the whole thing glues itself shut. Lay the second slice on top, butter side out, and cook in a heavy skillet over medium-low heat, lid on for the first three minutes, until both sides are deep gold and the cheese gives way, about four minutes a side. Open it a crack, drizzle in the rosemary honey, close it, press it once, and cut on the diagonal. Wrap it in waxed paper if you are taking it to the polls; eat it after you vote, not in the booth.
