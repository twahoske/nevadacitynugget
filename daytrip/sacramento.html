A Day Trip From Sacramento

Nevada City Nugget · Up the Hill and Back by Dark

◂ Today's Edition of the Nugget

An hour and a half up the hill from Sacramento sits a gold-rush town that still looks the part: a hotel from 1856, a theater from 1865, a creek walk below the old Chinese Quarter, and pine air. Here is how to do it in a day.

Getting Here · Parking · One Day · Seasons · This Week

Last checked October 29, 2026

Getting Here

  1. Take Interstate 80 east to Auburn.
  2. In Auburn, exit onto Highway 49 north toward Grass Valley and Nevada City, and follow it about 26 miles, straight through Grass Valley.
  3. In Nevada City, take the Broad Street exit, the one right after Sacramento Street, and turn left over the freeway. You are at the foot of Broad.

It is about 60 miles from downtown Sacramento. Allow an hour and a half, more on a Friday afternoon or a December Sunday. The road climbs to 2,477 feet, so mountain weather can find you; check Caltrans QuickMap before a winter drive.

Without a car. Honestly, it is hard. Nevada County Connects Route 5 runs between the Auburn Amtrak station and Grass Valley on weekdays only, six trips each way, $3 cash and exact change; only its first trip of the morning stops in Nevada City. There is no Sunday service. For a day trip, drive, or ride with someone who will.

Sources: Nevada City Chamber, visitor information; Nevada City Film Festival directions; Nevada County Connects Route 5, fares and service days.

One Day in Nevada City

  • Morning

    Coffee on Broad Street

    Park, then start with coffee and a bagel at Java John's, 306 Broad (from 7 AM daily), or eggs at Communal Cafe, 233 Broad (breakfast 7 AM to 2 PM). Pick up a walking map and a Chinese Quarter guide at the Chamber, 132 Main St. Java John's was co-founded by the publisher's husband, who sold it in 2002 and has no stake now.

  • Late morning

    Walk the old town

    Broad Street is the whole gold-rush town in four blocks. Look in on the Nevada Theatre at 401 Broad, open since 1865 and billed as California's oldest theater building still in use; the National Exchange Hotel at 211 Broad, from 1856; and Firehouse No. 1 at 214 Main, built in 1861, now a free museum of Nisenan, Chinese and Donner Party history. (The museum keeps Wednesday to Sunday, 1 to 4 PM, May through October; the rest of the year, by appointment.) The Hat Store is at 314 Broad, open daily 11 AM to 5 PM. The Hat Store is the publisher's own shop.

  • Noon

    Lunch

    Heartwood Eatery, 237 Commercial, for farm-direct bowls and salads. Taqueria El Taco Loco, 104 Union, open every day 10 to 9. Lika's Quarter Café, 401 Commercial (closed Tuesday and Wednesday), or Petra, 115 S. Pine, for Mediterranean bowls. Today's full grid, at three prices, is the Bill of Fare in today's edition.

  • Afternoon

    Walk it off

    The Deer Creek Tribute Trail follows Little Deer Creek from Pioneer Park on Nimrod Street to the foot of Broad and up through the old Chinese Quarter: two nearly flat miles, never far from a café. With more time and a car, the Independence Trail runs level above the South Yuba canyon, 6.25 miles north on Highway 49. Every walk we know is in Trails & Vistas.

  • Late afternoon

    A glass of something

    Nevada City Winery pours at 321 Spring St. every afternoon, and Szabo Vineyards at 316 Broad, Friday to Sunday. The rest of the town's bars, from the saloons to the cocktail rooms, are in the Bar Guide. Szabo is next door to The Hat Store; there is no business tie.

  • Evening

    Dinner, then home or a bed

    Lola at the National Exchange for a big night ($$$), Three Forks at 211 Commercial for pizza and house beer ($$), or Friar Tuck's at 111 N. Pine, dinner only. It is an hour and a half back down the hill in the dark, so pick a driver, or stay over: the inns are in Where to Stay.

Hours change with the seasons. Call before you count on any of them.

Sources: hours and addresses are as each place posts them, gathered in the Nugget's Bar Guide, Trails & Vistas and daily Bill of Fare; Nevada Theatre; Nevada County Historical Society, museums; Firehouse No. 1 build date from Wikipedia.

By the Season

Victorian Christmas, December 2026

The big one. Sundays, December 6, 13 and 20, 1:30 to 6 PM, and Wednesdays, December 9 and 16, 5 to 9 PM. Broad and Commercial close to traffic and downtown parking is gone, so use the shuttles: park at the Rood Government Center, 950 Maidu Ave. (drops you at the top of Broad, wheelchair accessible) or at Bodhi Hive, 420 Providence Mine Rd. (drops you at the bottom). $8 round trip, kids under 15 free, costumed riders free. Don't park on the freeway ramps; they tow. Source: Nevada City Chamber.

Winter

Mostly cool and mild; the Chamber says town sees a few inches of snow a handful of times a year. The history museums go to appointment only from November through April. Sources: Chamber, Historical Society.

Summer

Warm and dry, with July highs around 87, which is when Sacramento comes up for the river. Start a river walk early and carry water. Source: Chamber.

On the way: Empire Mine, Grass Valley

Highway 49 runs right past the old Empire Mine, now a state historic park at 10791 E. Empire St.: 10 AM to 4 PM from November through February, 10 to 5 the rest of the year, $5 for adults. Source: California State Parks.
