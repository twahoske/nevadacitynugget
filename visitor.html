After Dark

The Nevada City nightlife guide

Find them in the directory Downtown bars Downtown stages The Onyx & Seven Hills

Nevada City is a small town that stays up later than it looks. Nearly all of this is within two blocks of Broad and Commercial, so one parking spot covers the night.

Live MusicGolden Era, 309 Broad: live music most weekends, often on Fridays, says the publisher. Crazy Horse Saloon, 230 Commercial: live bands. The Fern, 235 Commercial: shows and DJ nights, usually from 9 PM Fridays and Saturdays. Trailhead Tavern, 315 Broad: live acoustic music Fridays, a DJ Saturdays. Friar Tuck's, 111 N. Pine: live music with dinner.
Games & SingingTrailhead Tavern: trivia Tuesdays, karaoke Wednesdays, Jeopardy Thursdays, Music Bingo Sundays.
Open LatestThe Fern and, on weekends, Crazy Horse pour until 2 AM. Mine Shaft Saloon, 222 Broad, runs past 1 AM every night.
No AlcoholElixart, 203 York: a non-alcoholic bar and lounge with elixirs, kava and tea, open to 1 AM Tuesdays, Fridays and Saturdays per its Google listing.
A ShowNevada Theatre, 401 Broad, and Miners Foundry, 325 Spring, for plays, concerts and films; Off Broadstreet, 305 Commercial, for musical comedy with wine and dessert at cabaret tables; The Onyx Theatre, 107 Argall Way, for movies. What is on tonight is in today's paper under Amusements.

Trails & Vistas

Trail of the Day · Hoyt Trail to Hoyt Crossing

Hwy 49, 6.9 miles north; paved lot just before the highway bridge · 0.75 mile to the river · 200 ft · dirt · Easy to moderate

Cross the old bridge at the Highway 49 crossing and walk three quarters of a mile to Hoyt Crossing on the South Yuba, a boulder bar with swimming holes. The main beach is clothing-optional; no glass, no alcohol.

More hikes, trailheads, and current closures: Trails & Vistas, the Nugget's walker's guide. Conditions change, and winter rain can leave dirt trails muddy; call South Yuba River State Park, (530) 273-3884 before you go. Source: Bear Yuba Land Trust.

Spotlight on Downtown Nevada City

Broad Street, Commercial Street & the Blocks Between

Downtown Nevada City is a National Register historic district, most of it brick put up after the fires of the 1850s and '60s, and nearly all of it a short, steep walk from one end to the other. Broad Street climbs the hill, Commercial runs below it, and Pine, Main, York, Spring and Union fill in the blocks around them. Park once and go on foot. Spotlight on Seven Hills

Nevada City's Other Business District

About a mile south of Broad Street, tucked off Zion Street behind SPD Market, the Seven Hills District is where Nevada City does its errands, and a good deal more. The Chamber of Commerce counts an arthouse movie theater, artisan food shops, casual places to eat, and more than 35 health practitioners along Zion Street, Argall Way, Searls Avenue and Providence Mine Road. Where to Park

Public parking in downtown Nevada City

Downtown is small and steep, so park once and walk. These are the city's public lots, each a block or two from Broad Street.

Spring StreetSpring Street lot, 205 Spring St. Behind the National Exchange Hotel. Free, 3 to 4 hours.
Nevada StreetNevada Street lot, 101 Nevada St. Free, 4 hours.
Commercial StreetCommercial Street lot, 412 Commercial St. Metered, 4 hours. Public restrooms and EV chargers.
On the StreetDowntown meters cost 50 cents an hour, as the city posts. The Chamber's visitor center at 132 Main St. keeps change. ParkMobile works too, for a 15 percent fee (75 cents at least).
All DayStaying past four hours? The city suggests Coyote Street past Washington, upper Spring Street, Bennett Street or High Street.

Victorian Christmas changes everything: streets close and the shuttles are the way in. See the Victorian Christmas Guide. Rates and limits last checked Oct. 29, 2026; the signs on the spot win.

Sources: City of Nevada City, Parking; Nevada City Chamber, public parking and restrooms.

The Necessary

Public restrooms in downtown Nevada City

"The necessary" is what the 1800s called the privy, and every visitor ends up asking where it is. These are the public ones downtown, as the Nugget's publisher knows them. No purchase needed.

By the ChamberRobinson Plaza, 132 Main St. Outdoor public restrooms beside the Chamber of Commerce, at the foot of Main Street.
Commercial StreetCommercial Street parking lot, 412 Commercial St. Outdoor public restrooms in the city lot, behind the Remedy Garden shop.
City HallCity Hall, 317 Broad St. Weekdays only, when City Hall is open. The publisher calls it the best choice in town.

The Nugget has not found posted hours for the two outdoor restrooms, so it prints none. During Victorian Christmas there are extra facilities; see the Victorian Christmas Guide. Official public restrooms only; shops and restaurants keep theirs for customers.

Locked, moved, or one we missed? news@nevadacitynugget.com. Sources: the publisher, Nov. 21, 2026; Nevada City Chamber, public parking and restrooms.
