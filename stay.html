Where to Stay

Nevada City Nugget · Hotels, Inns, Cottages & Camps

Nevada City is small enough to walk end to end, and its grandest hotel dates to 1856. Book early for Victorian Christmas and summer weekends. Details are as each place posts them; call before you count on anything.

Booking a house instead? Read Vacation Rentals & Measure Q below.

Last checked October 28, 2026

Right Downtown

Park once, walk to dinner, walk back.

  • The National Exchange Hotel211 Broad St.

    38 rooms · (530) 362-7605

    Broad Street's grand old hotel, built in 1856 and restored, with Lola for dinner and brunch, the historic National Bar, and a balcony over the street. Source: thenationalexchangehotel.com.

  • 1856 Speakeasy Suites233 Broad St.

    Nine rooms, each with its own bath

    Rooms remodeled in 2023 inside a brick gold-rush building that began as a saloon and boarding house, later the U.S. Hotel B&B. Book one room or all nine for a group; there's a café downstairs. Source: the1856speakeasy.com.

  • The Parsonage 1865427 Broad St.

    Ten bedrooms in the house and bungalow · (530) 798-6061

    The Methodist church's old parsonage, once the Madison House B&B and now a Victorian guest house with courtyard and tiered gardens. Rent a room or the whole place for a wedding party or reunion. Small pets only, and only in the Bungalow. Source: theparsonage1865.com.

  • Two Room Inn431 Broad St.

    A whole Victorian cottage · book online

    Two bedrooms behind antique stained glass, with a kitchen, washer and dryer, front porch, and private parking. Sleeps a family. Source: tworoominn.com.

  • Moonrise on Mill St.107 Mill St.

    Two houses, sleeps up to 11 · (530) 264-8810

    A restored 1890s Victorian split into The Residence and The Carriage Haus, with original trim, wide-plank floors, and a shared courtyard, a block off Broad. Source: moonriseonmillst.com.

  • Broad Street Inn517 W. Broad St.

    Six rooms plus a carriage house · (530) 265-2239

    A family-run 1870 inn at the quiet top of Broad, with a garden and rooms done up green and contemporary. The carriage house has a full kitchen. Source: broadstreetinn.com.

A Short Walk Out

Quieter streets, ten minutes or so from Broad.

  • Piety Hill Cottages523 Sacramento St.

    Cottages on an acre · (530) 265-2245

    A cluster of cottages on park-like grounds up Sacramento Street, an easy walk into town. Pet policy posted on its site. Source: pietyhillcottages.com.

  • Outside Inn575 E. Broad St.

    15 rooms, a cabin and a cottage · (530) 265-2233

    A family-owned small hotel with themed rooms, a summer pool, a creekside patio, and free trail and swimming-hole maps at the office. Dogs welcome in 14 of 15 rooms ($25 a dog a night, posted). Source: outsideinn.com.

  • Flume's End317 S. Pine St.

    Five rooms, a cottage and a yurt · (510) 779-8083

    A four-story 1861 Victorian on almost two acres along Gold Run Creek, with the old water flume on the grounds, balconies, a creekside fire pit, and off-street parking. Breakfast offered. Source: flumesend.com.

  • Northern Queen Inn400 Railroad Ave.

    71 rooms, cabins and chalets · (530) 265-5824

    Sixteen pine-covered acres a few minutes from downtown, with motel rooms, suites, and cabins and chalets with kitchenettes and porches. Pet-friendly. Source: northernqueeninn.com.

Camp & RV

Pitch a tent or back in the rig. Fire rules change with the season; ask when you book.

  • Inn Town Campground9 Kidder Ct., about two miles from downtown

    Tents, RV hookups, glamping tents · (530) 265-9900

    Locally owned, on 15 forested acres, with hot showers, laundry, a camp store, a communal kitchen, Wi-Fi, and a pool. Dog friendly. Source: inntowncampground.com.

  • Scotts Flat Lake23333 Scotts Flat Rd., about nine miles east off Highway 20

    Open year-round · (530) 265-5302

    The Nevada Irrigation District's lake campground at 3,100 feet. Posted 2026 rates: tent sites $57 a night, RV sites $68, more on the water; two leashed pets per site. Wood and charcoal fires are banned in fire season. Reservations for 2027 open January 2. Source: Nevada Irrigation District.

  • South Yuba CampgroundNorth Bloomfield Rd., about ten miles north

    16 sites · first come, first served · closed for winter

    A small Bureau of Land Management campground above the South Yuba River, $15 a night at the self-pay box, with piped water. Closed for the season on October 15, 2026; reservations through Recreation.gov begin with summer 2027. Big rigs take the Tyler Foote route; the Edwards Crossing bridge has a 4-ton limit. Source: Bureau of Land Management.

  • Malakoff Diggins State Historic ParkChute Hill Campground, 26 miles northeast

    Reservation only · park office (530) 265-2740

    Camp beside the hydraulic-mining moonscape, or book one of the cabins in the old town of North Bloomfield. No showers; trailers 19 feet and under. Book through ReserveCalifornia; the park listed no open reservations when we checked. Source: California State Parks.

  • Harmony Ridge Resort10492 Harmony Ridge Rd., three miles out

    Members-only resort · open year-round

    A family-owned private campground since 1985, with RV hookups, tent sites, log cabins, a pool and hot tub. It bills itself as members-only, so ask before you drive up. Source: harmonyridgeresort.com.

A Short Drive Out

Up Highway 20 into the pines, or down to Grass Valley when town is full.

  • Harmony Ridge Lodge18883 Highway 20

    B&B suites · (530) 478-0615

    Rustic-luxury guest suites on three and a half wooded acres up the ridge east of town, with a continental breakfast. Source: Nevada City Chamber lodging listings.

  • Gold Miners Inn121 Bank St., Grass Valley

    (530) 477-1700

    A downtown Grass Valley hotel set up for business travel, conferences and events. Source: Nevada City Chamber lodging listings.

  • Grass Valley Courtyard Suites210 N. Auburn St., Grass Valley

    42 rooms and suites · (530) 272-7696

    A boutique inn with an events center in an old brewery building and a poolside day spa. Source: Nevada City Chamber lodging listings.

  • Best Western Gold Country Inn972 Sutton Way, Grass Valley

    (530) 273-1393

    The chain option between the two towns, with breakfast, a fitness room, and an outdoor pool and spa. Source: Nevada City Chamber lodging listings.

Vacation Rentals & Measure Q

How Many Vacation Rentals Does Nevada City Have, and What Would Measure Q Change?

About 71 permitted short-term rentals, most of them whole houses · Voters decide November 3 whether to cap them

Plenty of visitors skip the inns and book a house. In Nevada City that means a short-term rental registered with the city, and how many of those there should be is the one city question on the November 3, 2026 ballot.

How many there are. City staff counted about 71 permitted short-term rentals this summer: 53 whole houses and 18 hosted rentals, where the owner lives on site, The Union reported in July. (In May, city officials had put the count of permitted hosted rentals at 62, with 62 to 73 likely operating, so the count depends on who is counting and when.) The city's software estimates the rentals bring in about $300,000 a year in hotel tax, the transient occupancy tax every overnight guest pays.

The rules today. A host registers with the city each year, holds a business license, files the hotel tax every quarter (Airbnb collects and pays it automatically; VRBO hosts pay it themselves), notifies the neighbors, and posts the city's Good Neighbor guidelines inside. Because voters enacted those rules by initiative, only voters can change them. The city planner told the council there are "no operational standards to enforce" beyond whether a rental is registered.

What Measure Q would do. According to the city attorney's impartial analysis, a yes vote repeals the registration rules and requires a permit to advertise or run any short-term rental, in two kinds: whole-home rentals and hosted rentals. New permits could not go within 300 feet of another permitted property. New whole-home permits would stop at 2 percent of the city's housing stock for main houses and 1 percent for accessory units, a cap the analysis expects to block new whole-home permits for main houses for now. Permits would end when a property sells or sits unused, a contact within 15 miles must answer the phone around the clock, and events, camping and subletting would be barred. Today's registrations get a one-time chance to convert, even past the cap. Unlike today's rules, the council could amend the new ones. A no vote keeps the current rules.

Where it leads. City staff told the council whole-house rentals would drift down from 53 to about 43 over time, and that the hotel-tax take would fall with them. The council put the measure on the ballot 4-0 in July, with Council Member Doug Fleming absent. Mayor Daniela Fernández and Council Member Adam Kline signed the ballot rebuttal in favor, which says the measure follows nearly three years of workshops and is not a ban. A part-time resident who rents out her Boulder Street home told the council the cap leaves no workable path to a new permit, The Union reported, and Council Member Micayla Sortland, a former rental operator who voted to send the measure to the ballot, called rental income a "safety net" for owners. The Nugget could not read the scanned text of the official argument against.

For visitors. Whichever way the vote goes, the rentals already registered keep operating while they stay in good standing, and every stay in town carries the city's hotel tax. Results will be added here after the election.

To vote. Ballots go in any official drop box, including the 24-hour box at City Hall, 317 Broad St., until 8 PM on Tuesday, November 3, or in person at the Rood Center vote center, 950 Maidu Ave.: 9 AM to 5 PM through November 2, and 7 AM to 8 PM on Election Day.

Sources: City Attorney's impartial analysis of Measure Q; City of Nevada City, Short-Term Rentals; Nevada City resolution placing the measure, July 23, 2026; Rebuttal to Argument Against Measure Q; Argument Against Measure Q; The Union, May 19, July 22 and July 25, 2026; Nevada County Elections, drop boxes and vote centers.

Before you go. Downtown parking is tight and the sidewalks are old brick and steep in places, so ask your host where to leave the car. Mountain weather turns fast; check the roads before a winter drive up.
